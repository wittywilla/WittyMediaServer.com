Supported formats

This page is made from the server’s own list of formats (internal/mediatypes), so it always matches what the server does. The same list is in the app, under Settings, About, Supported formats, where it also says what your own FFmpeg can do, and at GET /api/media-types for modules and apps.

A file’s content decides, not its name. A file with a missing or wrong extension is recognised from its first bytes. A name that other things also use, such as .ts, is checked the same way. A file whose content clearly isn’t media, like a program named film.mkv, is left out of the library and the log says why.

FFmpeg does the decoding. Patent-encumbered codecs such as H.264, HEVC and AAC are handled by the FFmpeg on your machine, as with any program that uses it. Which ones you can use depends on your FFmpeg and where you live.

Video#

A file plays directly when your player can open its container and decode what’s inside. If only the container is wrong, the server rewraps it without touching the picture. If only the sound is wrong, it converts the sound alone. The picture is only converted when nothing simpler would play.

Special cases#

  • HDR10, HDR10+, HLG and Dolby Vision play as they are on a player and screen that show them. Anything else is converted to fit an ordinary screen. HDR10+ plays its HDR10 layer unless the player handles more. Dolby Vision profile 5 has no plain HDR layer, so its colours may look a little off once converted.
  • 10-bit and 12-bit video plays directly where the player decodes it, and is converted elsewhere.
  • Interlaced video is deinterlaced as it’s converted.
  • Variable frame rates and damaged timestamps play: the stream is rebuilt as it’s converted.
  • 3D films in a frame (side by side, or one above the other) play as they are. The player’s information offers to show them in 2D.
  • Files with several video streams play the first, and the player’s information offers the others.
  • Cover art inside a video file is used as artwork and is never mistaken for the picture.
  • Films split across files (Film CD1.avi, Film CD2.avi, or Film.part1.mkv) are one title that plays straight through.
  • CUE sheets split one long sound file into the tracks of an album.
  • Live photos and motion photos play their clip, either beside the picture or inside its file.
  • SVG pictures lose anything active before they are sent.
