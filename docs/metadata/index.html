Where details come from

Learn how the server finds titles, plots, posters and ratings for what’s on it, what each source needs and what it’s sent, and what the optional logos and streaming listings mean. Most of it works with no account or key.

The order sources are used in#

Sources are used in this order, and later ones only fill gaps:

#SourceExample
1<file>.metadata.json next to the file{"title":"My Film","year":2001,"overview":"…","genres":["Animation"],"poster":"https://…"}
2NFO fileMy Film (2001).nfo, movie.nfo, tvshow.nfo, episode <name>.nfo
3Local artworkposter.jpg, <name>-poster.jpg, folder.jpg, fanart.jpg, season01-poster.jpg, <episode>-thumb.jpg
4File and folder namesMy Film (2001).mkv, Show - S01E02 - Title.mkv, MY_FILM.ISO (disc label)
5Your custom agents, in their orderAnything your own service knows
6TMDB and TheTVDB, if you added a keyOverview, genres, rating, network, posters, backdrops, episode titles
7TVmaze, for TV, until you add a TheTVDB keyOverview, genres, network, status, posters, backdrops, season posters, episode titles and stills
8Wikidata and Wikipedia, for films, until you add a TMDB keyYear, genres, US rating, runtime, plot summary, and a poster from Wikimedia Commons where the film has a free one (credited under Settings → Legal)
9AniList, for Anime librariesTitles in your chosen language, plots, posters, episode numbering
10A generated thumbnailA frame 20% into the video

Audio CDs have an order of their own, and games get theirs from WittyPlay: see Audio CDs and Video games.

An id in a file or folder’s name wins over any search, and a match you chose or a field you locked is never changed by any of these.

Content ratings. TVmaze and Wikidata don’t always have a content rating, which Kids and Guest profiles rely on. Set one under Edit on any film or show. A show’s rating covers its episodes.

API keys#

TV shows and films get information without any account or key. Adding a key later upgrades titles the next time they’re scanned or refreshed.

SourceNeedsGood forLicence

Wikidata and WikipediaNothingFilms and games: facts, ratings, plot summariesWikidata is public domain, Wikipedia text is CC BY-SA 4.0
AniListNothing. Always on for Anime libraries, which send it anime titlesAnime

To add a key:

  1. Open Settings → Metadata agents, under Built-in agents.
  2. Paste the key, and press Test to check it with the service.
  3. Press Save.

Saved keys are hidden, so paste a key again to test it. TVmaze and Wikidata can be turned off there, and title pages credit their source.

NFO files and artwork#

The server reads and writes the standard NFO and artwork layout most other media software uses, so you can move a library in or out without re-tagging anything.

An NFO can be up to 1 MB and a .metadata.json up to 256 KB. Anything read from beside your media, yours or anyone else’s, is cleaned to plain text: no HTML, scripts or hidden characters, and pictures only as web addresses or files in that folder. A file that can’t be read is left out, with a warning under Settings → WittyLog naming the file and the line.

More on what’s safe to change, and how it’s checked, is in Files beside your media.

What is saved next to your media#

Your metadata is always in the server’s database, and comes along in a backup.

With Save information and artwork next to your media on, which is the default, the server also writes these:

MediaWritten
Film or disc image in its own folder<name>.nfo, poster.jpg, fanart.jpg
Films sharing a folder, such as a folder of ISOs<name>.nfo, <name>-poster.jpg, <name>-fanart.jpg
VIDEO_TS foldermovie.nfo, poster.jpg, fanart.jpg inside the film’s folder
TV showtvshow.nfo, poster.jpg, fanart.jpg, season01-poster.jpg and so on, in the show’s folder
Episode<name>.nfo, <name>-thumb.jpg

Artwork saved this way is always a real picture from the internet, the image cache or your own disk. A file beside your media or a feed can’t make the server copy another kind of file here, or fetch something from your local network.

Scans only write for titles that have no NFO yet. Your own NFOs are never touched by a scan. If you edit a title whose NFO you wrote by hand, the original is kept as <name>.nfo.bak. Mount media read-only and turn the option off if you’d prefer the server not to write there.

Editing and fixing matches#

Fixing wrong details covers all of this: the Edit dialog, choosing a match, locks, Fix matches, renumbering episodes and naming rules. The metadata folder does the same with plain text files.

Audio CDs#

CD images have their own order: the file beside the image (<name>.wittycd.json, saved earlier), then CD-TEXT and the cue sheet, then folder and file names, then MusicBrainz, which fills in album and track names and a cover from the Cover Art Archive when it has one clear answer. Pictures beside the image always win.

What is sent. To look a disc up, the server sends MusicBrainz the disc’s ID and its table of contents, which is the track count and each track’s start. It says nothing about what the music is, what your files are called or who you are. Covers are fetched by the server, so nothing else learns your address. The lookup happens once for each new disc, at most one request a second, and Use MusicBrainz under Settings → Metadata agents turns it off.

Several matches. Pressings of one album share an answer, so any will do. If the matches disagree, or only the track lengths are close, nothing is guessed. The disc keeps its own names, and an administrator sees Choose from N matches on the CD player page, where you can pick the right one, change it later, or go back to the disc’s own names.

Saved beside your files? Only with saving on: a <name>.wittycd.json with the names and release, and a <name>-poster.jpg. With it off, the names stay in the server’s database.

Album data comes from MusicBrainz (CC0), and covers from the Cover Art Archive. Cover pictures belong to their owners. Both are credited on the player page.

Custom agents#

A custom agent is a web service of your own that the server asks about titles the built-in agents don’t know: a family archive, a club’s catalogue, a script you wrote.

  1. Open Settings → Metadata agents, under Custom agents, and press Add agent.
  2. Fill in the fields below.
  3. Under Try it, type a title and press Look up. That changes nothing.
  4. Press Add agent to save it.
FieldWhat it is
NameWhat to call it
AddressA web address with {title} in it, and {year} and {type} (movie, video or book) if you want them. For example, https://archive.local/lookup?title={title}&year={year}
API key headerOptional, sent with every request. For example, Authorization: Bearer abc123. It’s kept on the server
Use it forMovies, DVDs & Blu-rays, Home videos and Books

Agents run in the order listed, before TMDB, TheTVDB, TVmaze and Wikidata, and only fill in what’s still empty. Information inside your files, NFO files and your own edits always win. They’re also searched in Search and match, where an agent’s answer can be chosen like any other.

The agent answers with JSON: one object, a list, or {"results": [...]}. The first entry is used, and every field is optional:

{
  "id": "lx-2012",
  "title": "My Film",
  "year": 2012,
  "overview": "A lighthouse keeper finds a boat.",
  "tagline": "Some things come back.",
  "genres": ["Drama", "Mystery"],
  "rating": 6.4,
  "contentRating": "PG",
  "studio": "Lantern Works",
  "runtimeMinutes": 86,
  "poster": "https://archive.local/art/my-film.jpg",
  "backdrop": "https://archive.local/art/my-film-wide.jpg"
}

Answer 404 when you don’t know a title. Pictures are downloaded when the title is scanned. A picture on the agent’s own server is fetched like the agent, so it may be on your network. Any other picture is fetched from the internet only. Only real pictures (JPEG, PNG, WebP, GIF) are kept.

Categories, studios and browsing#

A film or TV library opens on shelves: categories, collections, studios, networks, recently added, highest rated, and a row for each category. Choosing a sort, a filter or a card turns it into a grid. The address holds the whole state, so any view can be bookmarked.

Categories are made from genres, one library at a time, with the art of what’s in them. Different spellings of a genre are put together (Sci-Fi and Science Fiction), and combined ones are split (Action & Adventure). Categories on the page lets an administrator rename, hide, reorder and merge them. Only the changes are kept, so new genres appear by themselves, and Start again forgets them.

Collections from film series appear by themselves. When a source says a film is part of a series, and two or more films in a library share it, they show as a card under Collections. The Collection field in the Edit dialog changes or clears it. For lists you make yourself, see Collections.

Studios and networks come from metadata, every studio a source names. A card opens everything from that studio, across libraries. Kids and Guest profiles see neither the titles they can’t watch, nor a studio or category only those titles are in.

Filters let you browse by almost anything: type, category, studio, network, collection, decade, year, age rating, score, picture size (4K, 1080p, 720p, SD), sound (such as AC3 5.1), audio and subtitle language, edition, tags, whether it’s been watched, words in the title, and streaming listings if they’re on. Save this view keeps a view for your account, and can pin it to Home.

Streaming listings#

This shows which of the titles already on your server a streaming service currently lists, for someone used to finding things that way. It’s optional, and off until an administrator accepts each statement of what it means.

Nothing plays from those services, no account with them is used, and there’s no connection with any of them. Every screen that shows a listing says so, and dates it.

To turn it on:

  1. Add a free TMDB key, as in API keys, and save.
  2. Open Settings → Metadata agents, under Streaming listings.
  3. Read each of the four statements, and tick it. They say that nothing streams from these services, that this project has no connection with them, that listings can be wrong or out of date, and that your server will send TMDB your titles.
  4. Choose your Region, and press Turn on listings.

Titles then show Also listed on on their page, and a shelf and filter appear on the library page.

SourceTMDB’s watch providers, which come from JustWatch. TMDB’s terms require JustWatch to be credited, which every screen does, and don’t allow commercial use
NeedsYour own free TMDB key. Without one it stays off
SentThe TMDB id of each title in your film and TV libraries, or its name and year to find that id, about once every two weeks. No files and nothing about you. A title that can’t be found with near certainty isn’t listed
What countsIncluded with a subscription, free, or free with adverts. Rentals and purchases aren’t counted
RegionYour choice, as listings differ by country. Changing it looks them up for that country
Kept up to dateA title you match, rematch or unmatch is looked at again straight away. Check now looks at everything again
Names and logosPlain text, unless you’ve agreed to logos too

Turning it off forgets everything it fetched.

Where to watch#

Apps can also ask where one title can be watched: GET /api/v1/media/{id}/where-to-watch for a title on the server, and GET /api/v1/discover/where-to-watch?tmdb=603&type=movie for any title on TMDB. The answer puts what’s here (in which quality, and every version) next to the services that offer it in your region, grouped as included with a subscription, free, free with adverts, to rent and to buy, each with its logo and a link to TMDB’s page of offers.

SourceThe same TMDB watch providers as the listings, credited to JustWatch on every answer (attribution)
NeedsYour own free TMDB key. Without one, the answer still says whether the title is here, with no services and reason: "no-api-key"
SentOnly the TMDB id of the title asked about, and the region. Nothing about you
RegionThe listings region, or US. An app can ask for another with region
KeptAs long as listings (two weeks). Check now under listings, or turning listings off, forgets them
Who sees whatSomeone who can’t see a title isn’t told it’s on the server

In the web app, a film’s or a show’s page has a Where to watch section:

  • In your library gives the quality of your copy, and lists every version when there are several, each a link to that version.
  • With a TMDB key, the services follow, under Stream, Free, Free with adverts, Rent and Buy. Each one links to TMDB’s page of offers, and the note under them says nothing plays from these services, names the country and the date, and carries the credit. Country looks at another country’s offers for this one title; it doesn’t change the server’s region.
  • Without a key, nothing about services is shown. An administrator sees one line saying where the key goes; other profiles see the section only for a title with several versions.

The services’ logos are the ones TMDB sends, so the browser fetches those small pictures from TMDB’s image server, without saying which page they are for.

Other sources can be added beside TMDB later without changing what apps get. See the client API.

The logo service#

This gives you logos for studios, networks, consoles, game studios and streaming services, in every part of the app. Nothing is bundled, and nothing is downloaded until an administrator agrees. Until then, and for anything with no logo, the app shows the name in plain text, and that’s a finished look rather than a gap.

Agreeing to logos#

Open Settings → Metadata agents, and find Logos. Everywhere a logo can be set, it explains this and links here until you’ve answered. Press I understand: download logos, or No thanks: use plain names. Here’s what you’re agreeing to:

  • Logos aren’t part of this software. They’re downloaded to your server, one at a time, from Wikimedia Commons.
  • Your server tells Wikidata and Wikimedia Commons the name of each studio, network, TV channel or console it finds, and nothing else.
  • Only files whose own page says they’re free to reuse are taken.
  • Logos are still trademarks of their owners. They’re shown only to name who made something, for your own use on your own server. If you share your server, that’s for you to judge.

Afterwards, Download logos turns it off, Remove them also deletes the pictures, and Show the list gives where each came from. Everyone signed in can see the same list under Legal, along with any channel wearing a picture from its channel list or its owner, which has no licence to state. Logos you uploaded stay. Uploading your own works without agreeing, since nothing is downloaded.

Where logos come from, and the terms#

SourceWikidata says which file is a company’s logo. The data is CC0. The file itself is on Wikimedia Commons, where each file’s page states its licence
What’s takenOnly public domain files and free licences that allow changes and commercial use (CC0, CC BY, CC BY-SA). Anything marked non-free, non-commercial or no-derivatives is skipped
CreditSource, licence and author are kept with each logo and listed under Show the list, which is what those licences ask for. The white and dark versions are recoloured, and the list says so
SentThe name, and nothing else, at about one request a second
TMDB’s logosNot used. TMDB’s API terms don’t allow derivatives, and logos have to be recoloured to read on any background

A logo is used without asking only if the name on Wikidata, or one of its other names, is the name in your library or, for a tuner or playlist channel, its name with the quality and country taken off. Anything close but not exact is only offered under Change, where a person chooses. See Logos.

What happens to a picture#

It’s read, limited to 8 MB and 25 megapixels, and written again from its pixels as a plain PNG, so nothing but pixels gets through: no SVG, scripts or metadata. Then two more versions are made, all white and all dark. If a logo has two clear tones, the larger tone is painted and the smaller is cut out, so the letters stay readable. A picture whose background can’t be taken off stays as it is, and gets a plate of its own.

Pictures are kept in the image cache and served only by this server, so no browser or app contacts the source.

Video games#

A game’s details are WittyPlay’s, not the server’s. A games library is scanned through WittyPlay’s scan hooks: the server walks the folders, WittyPlay says what each file is, and its scan.describe answer fills in the game’s title, summary, genres, makers, ratings, age rating and pictures. What it finds online later (libretro, Wikidata, IGDB, ScreenScraper, RAWG, TheGamesDB, RetroAchievements) it sets on the item through the host API, in the background, so a scan never waits on the internet. Which sources it uses, what each is sent and how a game is matched are in WittyPlay’s own docs.

The sources’ keys (IGDB, ScreenScraper, RAWG, TheGamesDB, RetroAchievements) are WittyPlay’s secrets: an administrator sets them in WittyPlay’s settings, which keeps them with PUT /api/modules/wittyplay/secrets/{name}. The server never shows a secret again, and only WittyPlay reads it.

What you typed stays. A field changed with Edit on a game’s page is kept by the server (item_edits) and passed to WittyPlay as edited, so no scan or lookup changes it. A picture or NFO beside a game still comes first.

Age ratings and Kids profiles#

ESRB, PEGI, CERO, USK, ACB, GRAC, ClassInd and ELSPA ratings all become a minimum age, in the server’s own ratings code. The strictest one a game has is its content rating, written like ESRB T, and that’s what Kids and Guest profiles go by, as for a film. A game above a profile’s limit doesn’t appear at all, and can’t be opened or played. A game with no rating counts as unrated, so it’s hidden from profiles that don’t allow unrated titles: set one with Edit on its page.

Cast and crew#

Every film, show and episode can list who is in it and who made it: the cast with the part each played, guest stars on episodes, and the crew by department (Directing, Writing, Production, Camera, Lighting, Editing, Sound, Art with production design, art direction and set decoration, Costume & Make-Up, Visual Effects, and the rest of the crew). Each person has a page of their own with their picture, dates, birthplace and biography, and every title of theirs in this library. Search finds people by name, and a library can be filtered to one person’s titles (person= on GET /browse, and in saved views).

Where the names come from. Each source’s list is kept on its own, and a page takes each part (the cast, the guest stars, each department) from the best source that has that part, in this order:

#SourceWhat it gives
1The NFO beside the file (movie.nfo, <name>.nfo, tvshow.nfo, an episode’s .nfo)<actor> with <name>, one <role> per part, <order> (or <sortorder>; without either, the order they are listed in), <thumb> and the person’s <tmdbid>/<imdbid>/<uniqueid type="tmdb">; <type> (an <actor> typed Director, Writer, Producer, Composer and so on is crew); <director> (repeated, or several in one as A / B), <credits> and <writer> (writers), <producer>, <composer>, <cinematographer>, <editor>, <productiondesigner>, <artdirector>, <setdecorator>, <costumedesigner>; an episode’s <actor> are its guest stars, except those the show lists as its cast
2TMDB (with a key)A film’s whole cast and crew; a show’s cast and crew and each episode’s guest stars and crew; each person’s picture, dates, birthplace and biography
3TheTVDB (with the owner’s key)A show’s people by type: actors, guest stars on their episodes, directors, writers, producers, creators
4TVmazeA show’s cast and crew
5The file’s own tagsA video’s DIRECTOR, WRITTEN_BY, SCREENPLAY_BY, PRODUCER, COMPOSER, ACTOR, DIRECTOR_OF_PHOTOGRAPHY, EDITED_BY, PRODUCTION_DESIGNER and COSTUME_DESIGNER tags (Matroska), and an MP4’s director (©dir); never its artist

So an NFO that names the actors but not the crew gets its cast from the NFO and its crew from TMDB. Reading a source again replaces only what that source said, and a source that couldn’t be reached (no network) leaves what it said before. An NFO that is removed takes its people with it when the title is next read: a show’s tvshow.nfo at every scan, a film’s or an episode’s NFO when its file changes or on Refresh metadata. A service’s people go with the title’s match: matching a title to another service, or taking its match away, drops what the old one said.

A new or changed file is read when it is scanned. Refresh metadata reads them all again: the service the title is matched to, the NFO beside it and, for a film or an episode, the file’s tags. Titles scanned before credits existed get theirs by themselves after each scan, a few at a time: the NFOs of up to 400 of a library’s films and episodes, the people of up to 25 of its matched films and shows from their service (only one whose key is set and that is switched on), and the pictures and biographies of the 10 most credited people nobody has looked up. Each title is asked about once, whether the source names anyone or not.

One person, many titles. People are kept once: by their number at TMDB, IMDb, TheTVDB or TVmaze when a source gives one, else by their name (ignoring case, accents and punctuation). Two people with the same name but different numbers at a source stay two people. Someone no title names any more is forgotten at the end of a scan.

Pictures. A person’s picture is a picture beside the media in an .actors folder, as media centres keep them (.actors/Ana_Lima.jpg, or .png, .webp: in a film’s folder, and in a show’s folder for the show and its episodes), the NFO’s <thumb> when it is a web address, or the one TheTVDB, TVmaze or TMDB has. It is fetched once into the image cache and made smaller for each size asked for, as posters are (GET /api/v1/people/{id}/photo?w=). A picture of the owner’s own beside the media is kept over one from the web.

Biographies come from TMDB, for people TMDB knows, when their page is first opened and then at most once a month; a paced background pass after a scan fills in the most credited people first. Nothing is asked for a person without a TMDB number, and nothing at all without a key.

What a profile sees. Credits are read only for a title the profile may see. A person’s page lists only their titles the profile may see; someone credited only on titles it may not see has no page and isn’t found by search, so nothing about a hidden title shows through its people.

Saving beside the media. With Save information beside the media on, the NFO the server writes holds the cast (<actor> with name, role, order, thumb when it is a web address, and the TMDB number) and the crew (<director>, <credits> for the writers, <producer>, <composer>, <cinematographer>, <editor>, <productiondesigner>, <artdirector>, <setdecorator>, <costumedesigner>), each part from its best source, so another media server reads the same names. An episode’s lists the show’s regulars (<type>Actor</type>) and its own guest stars (<type>GuestStar</type>), and only its own crew. Reading that file back names the same people, so nobody is doubled. It is never written while the setting is off.

For apps: People and the Credits component (SDUI.md).

Lyrics and artist information#

Music can also find lyrics, and artists’ biographies and photos, online. Both are off until the owner turns them on, under Settings → Metadata agents. What’s sent to whom is set out there and in Lyrics and karaoke.

Coming from another media server#

  • Software that uses NFO files: point a library at the same folders. Existing NFOs and artwork are read as they are, including TMDB and TheTVDB ids, so matches are exact.
  • Servers that keep metadata in their own database: local artwork (poster.jpg, fanart.jpg) is still read, and titles match by the usual naming (Movie (Year), Show/Season 01/Show S01E01). After a scan with saving on, your folders gain NFOs that other NFO-based tools can read too.

For developers#

Using logos in the web app#

import BrandLogo from '../components/BrandLogo'

<BrandLogo kind="network" name={show.network} />            // follows the theme
<BrandLogo kind="studio" name={it.studio} on="dark" />       // on a plate that's always dark, such as over artwork

It renders the logo, or the name in text, on its own. Lookups in the same moment go as one request. On a screen showing logos, also show <LegalNote kind="logos" /> once. useLogo(kind, name) and lookupLogo(kind, name) give the details, and forgetLogos() drops what’s been remembered after a change.

Kinds: studio, network, console, gamestudio, platform.

GET /api/modules/wittyplay/games/consoles gives each console a logoName, the name it goes by on Wikidata. On a games screen, use the marks made for this, which are the same size whichever way a console appears. With no logo they’re a short typed label:

import { ConsoleMark, StudioMark } from '../components/ConsoleMark'

<ConsoleMark console={c} />                     // sizes: sm, md, lg
<StudioMark name={it.studio} on="dark" />

Using logos from the server#

When you find a new console or game studio, call Store.EnsureLogo(ctx, kind, name). It only adds a row. Logos.Kick() starts looking, and does nothing without the owner’s agreement. The studios of films and networks of shows are added by scans already. Consoles and game studios are WittyPlay’s: it keeps their logos itself (/api/modules/wittyplay/games/brand-logos).

Logo routes#

RouteWhoWhat it does
GET /api/logos/statusSigned inconsent (yes, no or empty), source, sourceUrl, and for an administrator counts (pending, found, none, text)
PUT /api/logos/consent {accepted, removeDownloaded}AdminRecords the answer. Agreeing starts the downloads. Declining stops them, and removes what was downloaded if asked. Uploaded logos stay
POST /api/logos/lookup {logos: [{kind, name}]}Signed inUp to 200 names at once. Answers each with id, kind, name, status, has, version, opaque, source, sourceUrl, license, licenseUrl, credit. An admin’s lookup adds names nobody has asked about, and anyone else’s only reads, because a looked-up name goes to the internet
GET /api/logos/{id}/image?variant=white&v={version}Signed inThe PNG, or 404. variant is white, dark or original
GET /api/logos/creditsSigned inEvery logo with its source, licence and author. An account with limits gets the studios and networks of what it may see
GET /api/logos/{id}/candidates?q=AdminWhat the source offers, to choose from. 409 without the owner’s agreement
GET /api/logos/preview?src=AdminA candidate’s picture, through this server. Only Wikimedia addresses, and 409 until downloads are agreed to
PUT /api/logos/{id} {sourceId, q} or {status}AdminUse a candidate, or with status text the name alone, or with pending look again
PUT /api/logos/{id}/upload (the picture as the body)AdminUse your own. PNG, JPEG, WebP or GIF, up to 8 MB
GET /api/brands?library=&kind=studioSigned inThe studios or networks (kind=network) of a library, most titles first, counting only what this viewer may see

status is pending (not looked for yet), found, none (no source has one) or text (someone chose the name alone).

The ScreenScraper developer id#

ScreenScraper’s terms identify each program by a developer id. It belongs to WittyPlay’s ScreenScraper source, and is one of WittyPlay’s secrets (ssDevId, ssDevPassword), not part of the server.
