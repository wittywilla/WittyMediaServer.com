Watching and listening

Use this guide to get the most from the player: its controls and keys, tracks and subtitles, and what happens behind the picture. It also covers TV remotes, controllers, the mini player, playing on another device and watching together.

This is about the player in a web browser and the desktop app. The apps for phones and TVs have their own player: see Apps and devices.

Starting something#

Open a title and press Play. If you’ve started it before, the button says Resume from and the time, with Play from start beside it.

Discs have their own buttons. See DVDs and Blu-rays. Music, audiobooks and podcasts play in a bar along the bottom instead: see Music. Books open in a reader: see Books. Live channels have their own player: see Live TV.

The player’s controls#

Move the pointer or press a key and the controls appear. They hide again after 3.5 seconds unless you change that under Viewing settings.

Click the picture to pause or play. Double-click for full screen.

Along the top:

Back arrowGoes back to the page you came from, and keeps playing in the mini player
The title lineThe title, then how it’s playing: Direct play, Rewrapped or Transcoding
Info buttonOpens How this is playing

Mini player buttonShrinks the player into the corner

Along the bottom:

Play and pauseSpace does this too
Mute and volumeHidden on narrow screens, where the device’s own buttons do this

The speeds go from 0.5× up to what the server allows, which is 4× unless an administrator has changed it under Playback. Above 4×, most browsers mute the sound.

Sound tracks and subtitles#

Press Audio & language or Subtitles to open a screen of large tiles, one for each choice. A tick shows the one in use. Changing a track keeps your place.

  • Sound tracks show the language, and the format and channels.
  • Subtitles have Off first. Text subtitles are drawn by the player over the picture.
  • Subtitles stored as pictures, as on many discs, have to be drawn into the video itself, so choosing one means the server converts the video while you watch.
  • Styled subtitles can appear twice. The second tile says With its styling and Drawn into the picture. It keeps the fonts and placement, and the server has to convert the video to do it.

If subtitles can’t be loaded, the player says so and carries on playing.

Chapters#

The player calls chapters scenes. They show as small marks on the timeline, and Scenes lists them all with their start times. On a disc each one has a picture. Choose one to jump there.

Skip intro#

Skip intro appears while the intro is playing. Press it to jump to where the intro ends.

The server works out where the intro is from the file’s chapters. If it can’t, an administrator can set it by hand: open the title’s Edit, go to Skipping, and type where it starts and ends or choose Pick in player. Setting it on a show covers every episode with no intro of its own. See Fixing metadata.

Discs. A DVD or Blu-ray played as a disc (Insert, its menus, its titles) never shows these buttons and is never skipped by itself: the disc’s own program decides what plays and where its chapters are, each title has its own clock, and Next and Previous on the remote already step through its chapters. A film copied from a disc into a file is an ordinary video: it gets the skip buttons like any other, beside the disc remote.

Recaps, credits and adverts#

The server also finds a recap of earlier episodes, the credits, and advert breaks left in a recording, so apps can offer to skip them too.

It reads them from, most trusted first:

  1. What an administrator set by hand. No scan ever changes this.
  2. A .skip.json beside the file (Film.skip.json or Film.mkv.skip.json).
  3. An .edl beside the file (Film.edl or Film.mkv.edl).
  4. The file’s chapter names, in English, German, French, Spanish and romanized Japanese: Intro, Opening, OP, Vorspann; Previously on, Recap, Was bisher geschah, Précédemment, Anteriormente, Arasuji; Credits, End Credits, Outro, Ending, ED, Abspann, Générique de fin, Créditos.
  5. What comparing the season’s episodes found: see Finding intros nothing marks.
  6. The intro set for the whole show.

Each kind is taken from the most trusted source that has it, so an EDL’s advert breaks can sit beside a chapter’s credits.

An EDL is the MPlayer-style edit list many recording tools write: one range a line, as start, end and type, in seconds or as hh:mm:ss.ms. Type 3 (an advert) and 0 (a cut) are advert breaks; 1 and 2 are ignored. A word after the type names the range instead:

0       45.5    3   recap
62      151.5   3   intro
600     780     3
1290    1380    3   credits

A .skip.json says the same in JSON. A credits range without an end runs to the end of the file:

{"intro": {"start": 62, "end": 151.5}, "recap": {"start": 0, "end": 45.5},
 "credits": {"start": 1290}, "commercials": [{"start": 600, "end": 780}]}

A range that doesn’t make sense (one that ends before it starts, or a file that can’t be read) is ignored. Apps read them all from GET /api/v1/media/{id}/markers, and get them with the playback answer when something starts. The web player shows Skip recap, Skip intro, Skip adverts and Skip credits from the same list (credits that run to the very end are left to Next episode). An administrator sets them in the title’s Edit, under Skipping (see Fixing metadata), or with PUT on the same address, and {"reset": true} goes back to what the files say. See the client API.

A .wittyinfo beside the video (or for a whole season or show) says all of this and more: a preview of the next episode, a scene after the credits (skipping the credits then stops there), any other part someone named (“Skip dream sequence”), scenes in place of the chapters, and content filters. It wins over everything above, and a season’s or show’s file wins over chapters and what was found by itself. Each part says whether to offer a button, skip by itself, or only mark where it is. Edit it from a title’s ⋯ menu, Edit scenes and skips, or from the player’s ⋯ menu while watching, on a computer: TV mode and the TV apps play what a file says but don’t edit it. See the .wittyinfo format.

Skipping without pressing anything#

Each profile can have the player skip by itself: Settings → Playback → Skipping for an administrator, Settings → Appearance → Skipping for everyone else. There are six switches, all off to begin with: Skip intros, Skip recaps, Skip credits, Skip adverts, Skip previews and Skip other marked parts. A seventh, Do as the video’s file says, is on to begin with: a part a .wittyinfo says to skip by itself is skipped (with the same note and Undo); off, it gets a button instead.

Content, below them, says what to do about parts a video’s .wittyinfo marks as violence, strong language, nudity, sex, drugs, frightening scenes, flashing lights, discrimination or other: play it, warn, mute the sound while it lasts, or skip it, and only from a chosen strength up. Nothing happens for a profile that chose nothing, so a child’s profile can skip what an adult’s plays. The choice is kept with the account, so it follows you to your other devices.

When it skipsWhen playing runs into a part, or starts inside one. A short note says Skipped intro with Undo, and a screen reader reads it out
Once onlyEach part is skipped once. After Undo it plays, and it isn’t skipped again while the video is open
Going backSeek back into a part and it plays: you asked to see it
CreditsWith an episode after this one, the note counts down five seconds and goes on to it; Watch the credits stops that. With no next episode, credits are skipped only when something comes after them
Watch partiesOnly someone who may control the party skips, and it skips for everyone, as any seek does. Undo takes everyone back. What plays next is left to the party
DiscsNever: see above

Finding intros nothing marks#

Most files have no chapters and nothing beside them. For those, the server can find the intro by ear: the title music is the same in every episode of a season, so it compares the sound of each episode with the next and marks the stretch they share. It does the same at the end for the credits.

An administrator starts it for a show with Find intros and credits on the show’s page (under More on a phone), for every season or just one, or with POST /api/v1/shows/{id}/detect-intros ({"season": 2} for one season). It runs as a task. The window shows how many episodes have been listened to, can stop it, and says at the end how many episodes got an intro and how many got credits. Closing the window doesn’t stop it. Only one show is done at a time; if one is under way, the window shows that one.

What it listens toThe first 10 minutes of each episode for the intro, and the last 7 for the credits, of episodes that are files in a library. Discs are left alone
What countsA shared stretch of 15 seconds to 3 minutes at the front, 15 seconds to under 7 minutes at the back. A few seconds of an episode’s own sound over the titles doesn’t break it. Silence never matches silence
How it comparesEach episode with the next one, and with the one after that if the next shares nothing (a special, a recap episode)
TrustBelow chapters and anything a person set, above the show’s own intro. It’s listed with source: "detected"
CostFFmpeg decodes the sound only, at low priority, one episode at a time, and gives way while someone is watching. It hasn’t been timed on a Raspberry Pi yet. One run at a time on the server, 60 episodes a season at most
Running it againReplaces what it found before. An episode that no longer shares anything loses its detected markers; ones you set stay

It needs a season with at least two episodes, and it finds nothing for a show whose episodes each have different music, or none. It doesn’t find recaps: those differ in every episode. Nothing is sent anywhere, and no fingerprint is kept once the run ends.

What’s next#

In the last 30 seconds, a card offers what to watch next. At the end of an episode it counts down from 8 seconds, then plays the next episode. Dismiss stops it. After a film or the last episode, it suggests something else instead, and doesn’t start by itself.

Resuming and what counts as watched#

Your place is saved as you watch, about every 10 seconds, and when you leave. It belongs to your profile, so you can stop on one device and carry on from another.

  • Under a minute in: it starts from the beginning next time.
  • Past that: you can resume. It also appears in Jump back in on the home page.
  • More than 92% through: it counts as watched and gets a tick. Next time it starts from the beginning.

Library pages have a filter for Watched and Not watched yet.

Quality, direct play and converting#

Many video files can’t play on every device. Each time you press play, your device says what it can handle, and the server picks the simplest way to get it there:

What the player showsWhat it meansWork for the server
Direct playThe file is sent as it is. It’s the full quality, and jumping around is instantNone: your device does the work
RewrappedThe picture and sound are fine, but the file’s outer format isn’t, so they’re moved into one your device can openVery little
TranscodingThe picture or sound has to be converted while you watch. This is the only one that takes real workA lot

There’s no quality menu in the player. Quality comes from what the file is, what your device can play and the limits set by an administrator:

  • At home, under Live transcoding.
  • Away from home, under Limits for viewers away from home.

Occasionally a browser says it can play a file and then can’t. The player notices and asks the server to convert it, once, without you doing anything.

Robust streaming#

For a slow or unreliable connection: a hotel’s Wi-Fi, a phone on a train, a home connection that’s slow to send. It favours keeping the video playing over the sharpest picture. An administrator can turn it on for everyone away from home, under Playback, and each device can choose for itself, in the player’s viewing settings or on that settings page. A device’s own choice wins, at home or away, so it helps on poor Wi-Fi at home too.

With it on:

  • It measures your connection first, in about a second, and starts at a steady quality that fits in part of it: 1080p, 720p, 480p, 360p or 240p, never above the film’s own or the limits an administrator set.
  • It steps down instead of stalling. When the picture stops to wait, the player moves to the next quality down within a few seconds, and back up once things have been smooth for a minute.
  • It keeps a bigger buffer, a minute ahead rather than half that, so a short drop goes unnoticed.
  • It keeps trying when the connection drops, waiting a little longer each time, and picks up where you were once it’s back. If the server has ended the stream meanwhile, it asks for a new one at the same place.
  • Segments are two seconds long, each starting with a full picture, so changing quality or picking up again is quick.
  • A file that could play as it is is still sent as it is when it needs no more than half of what the connection carries. If it stalls for long anyway, the player asks for the steady stream instead, from the same place.
  • The player says what it did, briefly over the picture (“Picture lowered to 480p to keep playing”, “Reconnected after 12 s, at the same place”), and lists it under How this is playing.

It costs the server more work than standard streaming, since more videos are converted, but only one quality of each video is converted at a time.

How this is playing#

Press the info button, or I, to see this. It says what the file is, what’s happening to its picture, sound and subtitles, how much work that is for the server, and why it isn’t simpler. Use it first when a film stutters or won’t start.

Two choices can appear here:

  • Show it in 2D, for a 3D film stored with both eyes in one frame.
  • A list of video streams, for a file holding more than one, such as other camera angles.

See Supported formats for what plays and how.

Viewing settings#

The gear in the player. Apply to chooses This video or Everything. These are saved on this device, not on your profile.

  • Seek bar and Chapter marks show or hide those.
  • Remote and Remote style are for the remote on screen.
  • Hide controls after is 2, 3.5 or 6 seconds, or Never.
  • Picture is Fit, with black bars if needed, or Fill screen, which zooms and crops.
  • 3D video is for a film stored Side by side or Over-under. Show 3D as gives one eye’s view, or red and cyan for glasses.
  • Streaming is this device’s choice of robust streaming: Server’s choice, Standard or Robust. It applies to everything on this device, and changing it picks the video up again at the same place.

On discs there are also Forced captions, Disc restrictions and Subtitle size.

The remote on screen#

Remote, or R, shows a remote you can use with the mouse or by touch. You can drag it by its header, and it remembers where you put it. In control bar under Viewing settings shows its buttons in a strip instead. On a phone, it opens as a sheet along the bottom. Keys that only make sense on a disc are greyed out for ordinary videos. A film copied from a DVD or Blu-ray gets that disc’s remote, as The remote explains.

The mini player#

The back arrow or Esc shrinks a video into the corner while you carry on browsing. Esc leaves full screen first, if you’re in it.

The mini player has back 10 seconds, play and pause, next episode, Remote, Full player and Stop. A disc’s has previous and next chapter, Menu and Title too, which a phone keeps on the remote. Remote opens the remote over the page, so you can work a disc’s menu while you browse. Double-click the mini player or click its title to bring back the full player.

Music has a bar of its own along the bottom, which can fold away to a small picture. See Music.

Playing on another device#

Anything signed in to your server can be played on from anything else: choose a film on your phone and have it start on the browser by the sofa, or move what you’re watching to another screen.

  • Play on… is on every page something can be played from: a film’s, a show’s (the next episode, with the ones after it queued), a disc’s, an album’s (the whole album, as a queue), each song’s menu, a station’s, a book’s (Read on…), a game’s menu, and a picture’s bar. It lists your other devices, and offers Play here on those that play that kind of thing. A device that doesn’t says so.
  • In a player, Play on another device in the top bar hands over what you’re playing, at the same place with the same sound and subtitles, and stops it here. The video player, the disc player and the full music player have it; music takes its queue along.
  • Play on another device in the menu under your name opens the same list from anywhere.

The remote#

Remote beside a device turns this one into its remote control. It’s made for a phone held in one hand, and works the same with a keyboard, in TV mode and with a screen reader. It shows only what that device can do with what it’s playing:

  • what’s on, with its picture, and how far along it is. The line moves by itself between the device’s reports;
  • a bar to drag to another place. It moves there when you let go;
  • previous, back 10 seconds, play or pause, forward 30 seconds, next, and stop;
  • volume and mute, speed, and the sound track and subtitles, from the device’s own lists;
  • the queue: open it to see what’s coming, and pick an entry to jump to it;
  • a D-pad with OK, Back, Home, Menu and play or pause, which press those keys in the device’s own interface, as a TV remote would. So a phone can browse on the TV and start something without anyone touching the TV.

If something can’t be done, the remote says why in the device’s or the server’s own words: it isn’t connected, it didn’t answer, that kind of thing can’t be paused.

What a browser answers to#

A browser tab shows in the list while the app is open and showing in it, or playing something. It says it plays only what this server can play: a server without the DVD module has no browser claiming DVDs. Each player answers to what it can really do:

PlayingNext and previousAlso
A film or episodeThe queue it was sent, else the next episode. Previous goes back to the start firstEverything: seek, volume, speed, sound track, subtitles
A DVD or Blu-rayChaptersSeek and speed in a title, where the disc allows it; sound track and subtitles from the disc’s own. The D-pad works the disc’s menus, and Menu opens them
A CDTracksSeek within the track, volume
Music, an audiobook, a podcastIts own queueSeek, volume, speed
A radio stationThe stations queuedVolume. A station is live: no seeking and one speed
Live TVChannel up and downPause, back and forward within what’s been kept, volume, sound track, subtitles. A channel can’t be sent from another device, because channels aren’t library items: tune it on the TV, or with the D-pad
A book or comicPage turnsNothing else. It shows how far through you are
A pictureThe pictures either sidePlay and pause are the slideshow’s
A gameOnly stop. The game has the keys

A queue is up to 200 things, all videos (discs count) or all music. The video player plays one after another with its usual countdown, and a disc in a queue stays up until you move on, as a disc never ends by itself. Whatever is on screen is what a remote steers; with only music playing underneath, it steers the music.

You see devices signed in as your account. An administrator can let an account control devices signed in as others, such as a TV on a Kids profile, under Settings → Sharing & photos → Play on other devices. Nobody’s limits change: you can’t play something on another device that its account, or yours, isn’t allowed to see, and a device playing something you can’t see shows you only that it’s playing.

To turn it off for a browser, go to Play on… and switch off Let other devices play here. For how apps talk to each other, see the Remote Protocol.

Watching together#

Watch party on a title’s page starts a party, or joins one with a six-letter code. Everyone’s player then stays in step: a pause pauses it for everyone. See Watch parties.

TV remotes and controllers#

Everything works with arrow keys and OK alone, so a TV remote or a game controller can reach every control.

  • Arrows move to the nearest control in that direction. Coming back to a row lands where you left it.
  • OK presses what’s selected.
  • Back goes back a page, or closes what’s open.

In the player, the arrows belong to the picture: left and right jump 10 seconds, and up and down change the volume. Press OK to reach the controls. It goes first to anything asking for an answer, such as Skip intro, then to the controls. To give the arrows back to the picture, move up or down past the last control.

On a TV across the room, turn on TV mode under Appearance: the app as the Apple TV and Android TV apps have it, with Home, Libraries, Games, Live TV, Search and You along the top. On a TV browser with no easy way to reach Settings, add ?tv=1 to the server’s address. ?tv=0 turns it off again. In TV mode, up from a page’s first row reaches the bar along the top; Back goes from inside a page to the bar, from the bar to Home, and from a page opened from one of them back to it; and Back in the player stops it. For games on a sofa, see Couch mode.

Game controllers connect by themselves. Nintendo layouts have A and B swapped, so the right-hand button still confirms.

ButtonIn the appAlso in the player
D-pad or left stickMove
A or CrossOK
B or CircleBack
X or SquarePlay or pause
Y or TriangleMute, or a disc’s menu
BumpersIn TV mode, the tab to the left or rightPrevious and next scene on a disc

The activity button#

The round button in the top bar, just left of your name, shows the server’s background work, such as library scans. A ring around it shows progress and a number shows how many tasks there are. An exclamation mark means something had a problem.

Press it for two tabs: Now and History. Where a task allows it, you can Pause, Resume or Cancel it.

Only administrators see it unless an administrator allows others, who then get a read-only view. To hide it for yourself, or change who sees it, go to Activity. See Background tasks.

Moving posters#

Film and show posters lean toward your pointer as it crosses them, with a soft light moving over them. With a remote they settle in gently as you reach them.

To turn it off: go to Settings → Appearance → Motion and big screens and turn off Moving posters. The choice follows you to your other devices.

It’s off by itself on a device set to reduce motion or to save data, and on a very modest one, and that section says why. There’s no tilt on touch screens.

If it won’t play#

What you seeWhat to do
“Playback failed” and a reasonPress Back and try again. If it keeps happening, the reason is the thing to look up in Troubleshooting
“The stream has ended on the server”The server restarted. Start playing again
It stops and startsOpen How this is playing. If it says Transcoding, the server may not be keeping up: see Transcoding
It’s fine at home and stutters awayLower the limits for viewers away from home

Alpha software: it changes quickly and can fail or lose data. Keep backups, including your data folder, before you upgrade. No media comes with WittyMediaServer. Copy-protected discs, encrypted broadcasts and encrypted game files are refused with a plain message. Nothing here unlocks them.
