Minimum hardware

What kind of computer the server needs, in plain words, from figures measured on a deliberately small machine. What hardware it needs has the detail behind each figure, and Performance says how they’re measured.

Changed 24 November 2026 Edit on GitHub

  4. An old laptop or mini PC

  6. What needs a graphics card

In one line#

Any 64-bit computer with two processor cores and 2 GB of memory runs the server for a household, as long as the devices play the files as they are. Converting video while someone watches, separating a song’s voice for karaoke, and 3D game consoles are where a graphics card (or a much bigger processor) comes in.

The machine these figures come from#

A Linux container held to two processor cores and 3 GB of memory, with no graphics card, on the owner’s laptop (an Intel Core i7-12700H). Two cores of that processor are faster than two cores of a Raspberry Pi 4 or an old Celeron, roughly two to three times, so the times below are best cases for those machines; the memory figures are the same everywhere.

The library was 2,000 items made for the test: 1,500 films (20-second clips at 480p, 720p and 1080p) and 500 songs in 50 albums. A real film takes no longer to scan than a short one, since a scan reads a file’s details and one picture, not the whole file.

Measured on 24 November 2026, on the 0.8.4-alpha work (#643).

A Raspberry Pi or another small board#

A Raspberry Pi 4 or 5 with 2 GB or more, or a similar ARM board, running Linux.

  • Works well: browsing and searching the library, playing films and music the devices play as they are, music for a household, books, photos, live TV channels copied as they come, and recordings.
  • Works, slowly: the first scan of a big library. At a few films a second it is minutes for a few thousand titles, once; rescans read only folders and take under a second. Scans give way while someone is watching.
  • Not for this machine: converting video while someone watches (it took two cores of a much faster processor 2.2 seconds just to start a 1080p film), karaoke separation (many minutes a song), and 3D game consoles. Older consoles are drawn by the processor and need no graphics card; how many play at full speed on a Pi hasn’t been measured.
  • Not measured on a real Pi yet: these come from a two-core container on a laptop (above). A Pi’s cores are slower, so expect its scans and page times to take two to three times as long; its memory use is the same.
  • A Raspberry Pi 3 or anything with 1 GB works for a small music or book library; the server keeps itself to a few hundred megabytes there (small machines).

An old laptop or mini PC#

A dual-core or quad-core Intel or AMD from the last ten years, 4 GB or more: an old laptop, a Celeron, Pentium or N100 mini PC.

  • Works well: everything a Pi does, quicker; first scans of a few thousand titles in minutes.
  • Converting video: one 1080p film converted on the processor takes a bit over one core for as long as it plays. A dual-core manages one viewer; a quad-core two. A processor with Intel Quick Sync (almost every Intel since 2012, including Celeron, Pentium and N100) or an AMD with a graphics part converts in hardware instead, several streams at once, and the server uses it by itself when it’s there (Transcoding).
  • Karaoke separation on the processor took 65 seconds for a four-minute song on the 14-core laptop, so expect a few minutes a song on a dual or quad core; the quick version is ready in seconds.
  • Games: older consoles, Nintendo 64 and PSP are drawn by the processor; GameCube and Wii too on a Linux server, at a heavy cost; PlayStation 2, Dreamcast and 3DS need a graphics card (Gaming).

A NAS#

A Synology, QNAP, TrueNAS, Unraid, UGREEN or Asustor box running the server in Docker (Docker has a guide for each).

  • The server counts what the container is given, not the whole box: a container held to two cores and 2 GB is treated as a small machine and holds itself back to match. Since 0.8.4 this counts a processor limit too, not only memory.
  • Most NAS processors are low-power Intel (Celeron, Pentium, Atom) or ARM. On an Intel one, pass the graphics device to the container (/dev/dri) and converting video uses Quick Sync; without it, a NAS converts at most one stream, and slowly.
  • An ARM NAS is like a Raspberry Pi above: a fine library and direct-play server, not a converting one.
  • Keep the server’s data folder on the NAS’s fastest disk (an SSD if there is one): the database and pictures are read on every page.

What needs a graphics card#

WhatWithout a graphics cardWith one
Converting video while someone watchesAbout one core per 1080p stream; 4K HDR is out of reach (15 cores’ worth)Several streams at once, 4K HDR included, at a few percent of the processor (GPU first)
Starting a converted stream2.2 seconds to the first picture for a 1080p film on two coresSooner: the card encodes the first seconds faster than the processor (not timed for this page)
Karaoke separation (taking a song’s voice out properly)About a minute for a four-minute song on a fast 14-core laptop; several minutes on a small machineAbout 10 seconds a song (NVIDIA, or DirectML on any Windows card)
WittyPlay 3D consoles (Nintendo 64 at its best, GameCube, Wii, PlayStation 2, Dreamcast, PSP, 3DS)GameCube and Wii only on Linux, drawn by the processor: 60 frames a second took 1 to 3.5 of a fast laptop’s threadsAll of them
Everything else (browsing, scanning, direct play, music, books, photos, TV channels copied as they come)FineNo difference

The server finds a graphics card by itself, tries it with a tiny real job, and goes back to the processor on its own when the card can’t do something (Settings, Transcoding shows what it found).

What the server itself takes#

On the two-core, 3 GB container, with no graphics card:

Measured
Memory, idle, no libraries36 to 65 MB, depending on the run (about 60 MB most often)
Memory, idle, after scanning the 2,000 items74 MB (68 MB once Go has handed back what it can); Go’s own data, 10 MB
Memory at its highest while scanning72 to 80 MB
Processor while idle0.04 % of one core, waking about 400 times a minute
Start-up0.16 s on a new data folder, 0.07 s after
First scan: 1,500 films96 to 111 seconds (about 15 films a second): most of it is FFmpeg reading each file and making its picture
First scan: 500 songs15 seconds (about 35 songs a second)
A rescan with nothing changed0.3 s a library
Home, as the apps ask for itHalf a millisecond for the page, 3 ms for its rows
A library’s pageUnder a millisecond for the page; 2 to 5 ms for its titles and filters
A search across the library4 ms
The web app’s first download328 KB of scripts and 69 KB of stylesheets for Home, and the 350 KB font (compressed, once); a visit after that fetches about 24 KB
Scrolling a library of 1,500 films to the endAbout 2,200 component draws in the browser, for 1,600 cards

Kept in check on every change#

Every pull request is measured against budgets, so these figures can’t quietly get worse:

  • The web app: what a first visit to each kind of page downloads (web/bundle-budget.json, checked by web/scripts/bundle-budget.mjs).
  • The server: idle memory after a scan, a scan’s speed, what the main pages cost, and how long a converted stream takes to start, on two processors (scripts/perf/budget.json, checked by scripts/perf -budget).

A change that goes over fails the check and says which figure and by how much. Performance says what to do then.

Alpha software: it changes quickly and can fail or lose data. Keep backups, including your data folder, before you upgrade. No media comes with WittyMediaServer. Copy-protected discs, encrypted broadcasts and encrypted game files are refused with a plain message. Nothing here unlocks them.

This site has no cookies, trackers or analytics. Your choice of colours is kept in your own browser. How this site handles privacy. Change the colours.
