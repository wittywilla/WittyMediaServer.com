The family's logo: orbitw

Every logo, icon and background of WittyMediaServer, WittyMediaClient, WittyMediaPlayer, WittyReader, the modules and the website is drawn by one program, scripts/genicon, from the drawings in internal/brandart. Nothing is drawn by hand and no picture is edited after it is made: change a drawing, then run scripts/sync-brand.sh in each repository.

The logo is called orbitw: the W in its orbit.

Every form of every logo, rendered (SVG, PNG at 16 to 1024 pixels, JPEG; colour, black and white on transparency; icons and name lockups): brand/orbitw/README.md.

Contents: The idea · Construction · Colours · The name · Every form · Using it · Small sizes and one colour · Generating it · What is written where · How each format is made · Other designs · Changing it

The idea#

A home media server is the middle of a small system: the server, and the screens and apps around it. The mark is that system drawn as simply as it can be: a ring (the home, the orbit everything moves in), a dot riding in a gap of the ring (whatever is connected right now), and inside the ring the one thing that app is. The server holds the W, for Witty. Every app keeps the ring and the dot, so they read as one family, and differs in what is inside and in the colour of its dot.

AppInside the ringRingDot
WittyMediaServerthe Wlilac into violetamber
WittyMediaClienta screen on a stand, carrying a small ring with the Wviolet into sky bluemint
WittyMediaPlayera play buttoncoral into violetsky blue
WittyReaderan open bookmint into sky bluecoral

The server and the client are told apart at a glance: a round mark against a screen on a stand, and an amber dot against a mint one. Module icons sit on the same tile with a mark in their catalogue category’s colour and the server’s amber dot, since modules are part of the server.

Construction#

Every mark is designed on a 1024 square. Below, R is the ring’s centre-line radius (300 on the design square); every other measure is a fraction of it, so the mark keeps its proportions at any size.

The ring. A circle of radius R stroked 0.22 R wide with round ends, open over 48° centred on the top right (45° from the top). The dot sits in that opening, centred on the ring’s line at 45°, 0.19 R in radius, so it reads as the ring’s own piece pulled free. The W is one outline of four thick strokes 0.27 R wide through five points: the two outer tops, the two bottoms at a quarter and three quarters of its width, and the middle peak lower, at 36% of its height; the joints are mitred, bevelled where they would spike, and every corner is rounded. It is 1.07 R wide and 0.73 R high, a little below the ring’s centre so it sits optically in the middle.

The client is a screen 1.93 R wide and 1.4 R high with corners of 0.31 R, a neck and a round-ended foot; on it a small ring (0.5 R) with its own W and the mint dot. Its ring and W are cut out of the screen, so the screen’s gradient runs behind them. The player has a play triangle with rounded corners in the ring; the reader two pages meeting at the spine.

The tile. App icons put the mark on a rounded square (corners 22% of the side, the curve run a little longer than a circle’s, like Apple’s continuous corners) filled with the house theme’s dark slate and a soft glow of the app’s second colour behind the mark. On macOS the tile follows Apple’s grid (824 of 1024, with its shadow).

Clear space and fit. On every square the mark’s farthest point is 0.325 of the square from its centre: inside the 72-of-108 circle every Android launcher mask shows, and clear of iOS’s, tvOS’s and visionOS’s corners. Around a mark on its own, keep clear at least the dot’s diameter on every side.

The name#

The name is set as the website sets it: Inter Bold (700), letter-spacing -0.02em, Inter’s text optical size, with “Media” (or “Reader”) in the accent: WittyMediaServer, WittyMediaClient, WittyMediaPlayer, WittyReader. Pictures draw it from the same font files the website and web client serve (web/public/fonts, internal/brandart/fonts: Inter 4.1, SIL Open Font License), with Inter’s own kerning read from its GPOS table, so a generated name and the site’s are letter for letter the same: checked by overlaying the two at 100 pixels, where their widths agree to a tenth of a pixel. The site and the web client pin the name to Inter’s text optical size (font-optical-sizing: none on .brand) so this holds at every size. Other words in pictures (the line on the social picture) are Inter Regular.

Using it#

  • Use the files as they are. Don’t redraw, stretch, rotate, recolour (beyond the black and white forms), outline, add shadows or effects, or put the colour mark on a busy picture: use mark-white there.
  • The colour mark goes on the house theme’s dark or on white or the Light theme’s paper; the gradients are made to hold their contrast on both.
  • Don’t separate the dot from the ring, close the ring’s gap, or change the dot’s colour between apps: the dot’s colour is how the apps are told apart.
  • The server’s own logo (Settings → Appearance) replaces the mark inside a server’s screens, never the app icons.

Small sizes and one colour#

At 16 to 32 pixels the mark is drawn again, not shrunk: its box fills more of the tile, the lines are 25% heavier, and the client’s screen drops the W inside its small ring (the ring and dot carry it). The one-colour form (the ring, the W and the dot in one colour, cuts as holes) is what the macOS menu bar shows as a template image (macOS colours it for a light or dark bar; faint while the server is stopped), Android’s themed icon, iOS’s tinted icon, and the black and white forms above. The Windows tray shows the colour icon, grey while the server is stopped.

Generating it#

go run ./scripts/genicon -target kit -o .                         # every form of every logo, into docs/brand/orbitw
go run ./scripts/genicon -target client -o ../WittyMediaClient    # one repository's set
go run ./scripts/genicon -target client -o ../WittyMediaClient -check   # only check it is up to date
go run ./scripts/genicon -sheet sheet.png                         # every design side by side, at real sizes

Each repository has scripts/sync-brand.sh, which runs genicon with its own target, finding this repository beside it (or at WITTY_SERVER); --check fails if anything is out of date. internal/brandart’s tests fail if the web client’s icons here aren’t what the generator draws now.

How each format is made#

Everything starts as vector outlines (internal/brandart/geom.go): lines and cubic curves, built from circles, rounded rectangles, thick arcs with round ends, rounded polygons and the W’s single outline. A cut-out runs the other way round from the shape around it, so one non-zero fill rule gives every hole, the same in every format.

FormatHow
SVGEach layer becomes one <path> with its fill: a colour, or a linearGradient/radialGradient in the drawing’s own units (render.go).
Android vector drawableThe same path data; gradients as aapt:attr fills, which need Android 7, so the layers with gradients (the adaptive icon, the Android 12 splash) live in drawable-v26 and the older launch screen uses PNG marks.
KotlinImageVector.Builder with addPathNodes and Brush gradients (codegen.go), in ui/BrandArt.kt.
SwiftA function per mark that fills SwiftUI Paths with GraphicsContext shadings, in App/BrandArt.swift.
PNGRasterised with exact-coverage anti-aliasing (golang.org/x/image/vector); gradients worked out per covered pixel. Opaque where a store demands it (App Store, Play, tvOS back layers).
JPEGThe PNG on a stated background, quality 90: the big backdrops and the kit’s JPEGs.
ICOPNG frames for Windows’ sizes (16, 20, 24, 32, 40, 48, 64, 96, 128, 256), each drawn at its own size.
ICNSPNG entries for every macOS size (16 to 1024, at 1x and 2x), on Apple’s 824 grid with its shadow.
BMP24-bit, bottom-up, as Windows Installer and NSIS take for their wizard pictures.
Asset catalogsThe Contents.json for each icon set, image stack layer, Top Shelf and image set, written beside the pictures.

Words are glyph outlines from Inter (see The name). Module marks are distance functions on a 128 grid (modules.go), each an original drawing, never a console’s, disc format’s, service’s or studio’s logo, so their icons are PNG only.

Backgrounds start from one quiet scene (compose.go, wide.go), made to sit behind words without pulling the eye: the house theme’s night (or the Light theme’s paper), one soft violet light right of centre, four thin, faint orbits around it, each open at a gap like the logo’s ring with a small dot in the apps’ dot colours, and a sparse field of tiny, dim stars. Nothing sparkles or moves. Stars and dots never fall where words or icons go, and the left side, where screens put their text, stays the calmest. On full-screen backdrops the W in its ring sits faintly at the orbits’ centre. The Top Shelf, TV banners, the TV sign-in backdrop, the installers, the disk image, the macOS installer and the website’s hero and social picture are all that scene with the right mark or name on it.

DesignWhat it is

Changing it#

  • A colour: change the theme (the CSS, then scripts/design-tokens.py), or the mapping in looks in marks.go.
  • The design in use: DefaultConcept in marks.go, then every repository’s scripts/sync-brand.sh and go run ./scripts/genicon -target kit -o . here.
  • A new app: a Product with its name, a looks entry with its own dot colour, its drawing in each design, and a target.
  • A new module: its mark in ModuleMarks (modules.go), then WittyModuleCatalog’s scripts/sync-brand.sh.
  • A new size or platform file: the target’s function in targets.go; the tests check every target’s sizes.

internal/brandart’s tests check that every target builds in every design, that each picture is the size its platform asks for, that store and home-screen icons are opaque and parallax layers are not, that every mark stays inside the launcher’s safe circle, that the server and client marks differ, that cuts show as holes in the one-colour form, that ICO and ICNS files are well formed, that Inter’s kerning is applied, and that the web client’s icons in the repository are what the generator draws now.
