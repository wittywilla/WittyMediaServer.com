Blu-rays

Use this guide to add Blu-ray images and folders to your server, play them with their real menus in a browser, and decide about discs with Java menus. The module that does this is WittyBluray, an original implementation of the logic a player runs.

Adding discs#

  1. Go to Settings → Modules and add WittyBluray. WittyDisc, which reads disc images, comes inside it. See Modules and the store.
  2. Go to Settings → Libraries → Add library, and choose DVDs, Blu-rays & CDs. Images of films can also go in a Movies library, and images of a series in a TV shows library.
  3. Choose the folder that holds your discs, and press Add library.

It takes .iso and .img images, among those listed in Supported formats, and folders that hold BDMV, however its name is capitalised. A library can also be the BDMV folder itself. AVCHD discs and camcorder cards are read too, and folders that hold a BDAV recording play as a Blu-ray with no menus.

A scan recognises a Blu-ray by its structure, not its name, and records its label, the feature’s length, resolution, codec, languages, chapters, and whether it has menus. 3D discs get a “3D” genre. Without WittyBluray, a disc stays listed with nothing known about what’s in it, and playing it offers to add the module. Adding it scans your disc libraries.

Encrypted discs are refused. An AACS-encrypted disc is listed with this message, and can’t be played: “This disc is encrypted, so it can’t be played: encrypted media isn’t supported, and nothing here bypasses copy protection.” The check looks at the video itself, not at folder names. BD+ is never run. An unencrypted disc just plays.

Playing a disc#

On a Blu-ray’s page:

ButtonWhat happens
Insert Blu-rayStarts the disc like a player: first-play, studio intros and warnings, then the top menu. Optionally with the tray animation and drive sound
Play movie, or Play from the start on a seriesStraight into the feature
Resume fromBack to where you stopped
Main menuThe disc’s top menu, skipping the intros

Disc settings on the page, for administrators, chooses what’s on the disc, whether inserting it starts with the menu or the feature, and the insert effect. The defaults, the region, parental limits and country are under Settings → DVDs and Blu-rays. Pick the region your discs are from: a disc for another region shows its own message instead of the movie.

In a menu, the arrow keys, the on-screen remote, a game controller or a TV remote move between buttons, and OK presses one. The disc’s own program then runs, as its authors intended. Top menu returns to the top menu from the film.

Pop-up menus. When the film carries one, Menu opens it over the picture while the film keeps playing: the remote’s key, the Menu button at the top of the player or in the mini player, or M. The arrows and OK work it, and Esc or Menu closes it. Discs use these for chapters, sound and subtitle choices with their own artwork. Top menu always goes to the disc’s top menu.

Menu buttons make the disc’s own sounds, pages play their transitions, and menus that move on by themselves after a while do so.

A disc can forbid seeking or skipping at some points, as on a disc player. To ignore that, turn off Disc restrictions in the player’s viewing settings.

Which playlist is the film#

WittyBluray 0.4.0 and later rank every playlist of a disc and explain their pick, so a disc with hundreds of decoy playlists plays its film. The scan keeps the answer, and the disc’s page shows Why this playlist under the feature: one sentence, how sure the engine is, and under How the engine decided the reasons, the decoys left out, files read from the disc’s backup copy, and where the copy’s playlists, clip information and streams disagree. The page lists only playlists worth watching as bonus features.

  • Chapters come from the engine, which puts a chapter mark that lies outside its play item back where it belongs.
  • Editions. Two or three cuts of the same film are listed under Other versions.
  • Episodes. A TV disc’s episodes are listed in viewing order. With only a “play all” playlist, or a film made of episode-length parts, each episode starts at its own place in it.
  • Choosing another playlist, Use the engine’s pick and Rank again work as for DVDs: an administrator’s choice is remembered for the disc, for everyone, and survives rescans.
  • A playlist no title names. Obfuscated discs often reach their film only through the program’s registers. Play movie plays the ranked playlist as a title anyway, with its chapters, sound, subtitles and progress.
  • Older modules. With a WittyBluray from before rankings, the older rule picks the film.

A second opinion from libbluray#

If a libbluray is installed on the server, WittyBluray can compare its reading of a disc with libbluray’s. It is off until an administrator turns on Settings → DVDs and Blu-rays → Ask libbluray for a second opinion, and the row says whether a libbluray was found. Then Compare with libbluray on a disc’s page asks once, and the answer appears under How the engine decided. WittyBluray’s own pick still decides what plays.

  • libbluray isn’t shipped. On macOS and Windows WittyBluray loads it at run time if one is installed (libbluray.2.dylib, libbluray-2.dll). On Linux the module is a static program and asks libbluray only when built with -tags libbluray.
  • It is a library from outside the project reading the disc’s files, in the module’s helper process, which is why it starts off.
  • Encrypted discs are never handed to it, and nothing is decrypted: no AACS or BD+ library is loaded.

The remote#

Press Remote, or R, for the WittyBluray remote. Its button is in the player’s controls and in the mini player, at every screen size, and on a phone it opens as a sheet along the bottom. Its keys work as on the DVD remote, whose guide lists which keys work on which kind of copy, and why. On a Blu-ray:

  • Top menu goes to the top menu. Menu opens the film’s pop-up menu, or with none, goes to the top menu.
  • Red, green, yellow and blue are on the remote, and on F1 to F4. Only Java menus use them, so they’re greyed out otherwise.
  • During a Java menu, the arrows, OK, the digits, the colour keys and Menu all go to the disc’s own program, even though it lists no buttons of its own.
  • A copy of the film alone, such as an .m2ts file from the disc’s STREAM folder or an MKV made from the disc, gets the same remote with its menu keys greyed out and the line “This copy has no disc menus”. Its chapters, sound and subtitles work.

Sound, subtitles and chapters#

  • Sound: every track in the feature is listed, with its language and channels: DTS-HD Master Audio, TrueHD, DTS, Dolby Digital, Dolby Digital Plus and PCM. It’s converted for the browser.
  • Subtitles: the disc’s own subtitle pictures, drawn over the video at their exact positions and times, in any of the disc’s languages. Forced ones, which translate on-screen text, show even with subtitles off. Forced captions in the viewing settings turns them off.
  • Chapters: the playlist’s chapter marks.
  • Angles: scenes with several angles play the one you choose, with Angle.

3D, 4K and HDR#

  • 3D discs (MVC) play their 2D view, because FFmpeg has no decoder for the other. The disc is marked 3D in the library.
  • Side-by-side and over-under 3D, on discs or files made that way: the viewing settings have 3D video, and Show 3D as: one eye in 2D, or red and cyan for paper glasses.
  • 4K UHD discs are read like any other. With Maximum resolution on Source, they’re sent as 1080p, because converting 4K in software is beyond most machines. Choose 2160p (4K) under Settings → Live transcoding to send them as 4K. No real UHD disc has been tried.
  • HDR (HDR10, HDR10+ and the HDR10 layer of Dolby Vision) is mapped to an ordinary picture for the browser. An FFmpeg without the zscale and tonemap filters plays it unmapped: watchable, but flat. HDR screens haven’t been checked.

BD-J (Java) menus#

Many commercial Blu-rays write their menus in Java. This is off until you turn it on. With it off, or with no Java, these discs play their main feature directly, and chapters, sound and subtitles are still there in the player’s own controls.

To show those menus:

  1. Install any Java 8 or newer on the server, with its graphics libraries. Eclipse Temurin is a free build of OpenJDK. A stripped-down Java without graphics can’t run them. On Linux, install fontconfig too. In Docker, add both to the image.
  2. Go to Settings → DVDs and Blu-rays and turn on Run Blu-ray Java menus (BD-J). The page shows which Java it found.
  3. If it found none, or you want another, name it in the server’s config file as javaPath, or with -java or WMS_JAVA, and start the server again. It can’t be set from the web page, so that a web account can’t choose what the server runs. A location saved in Settings by an older version is ignored.
  4. Insert the disc.

Each viewer’s disc starts its own small Java process, with a 192 MB heap, which ends with the session. The disc’s graphics reach the player as pictures at up to 12 a second, so fast animation looks less smooth than on a hardware player.

A Java menu is worked with the remote’s arrows, OK and colour keys. A tap or click on the picture reaches the menu only when the disc’s program says it takes a pointer. Most don’t, and Java menus that need one are rare.

How far this has been tested. It has run with our own test discs and with open-source sample applications, not yet on a retail disc. None of the discs tried so far has Java menus. If an application can’t run, the disc plays its main feature instead.

Safety. A BD-J disc contains code. It runs in a sandbox with no network and no access to files outside the disc’s own areas, and a hostile test disc tries to break out every time the tests run. If a disc uses too much memory, too many threads or too much processor time, its Java process is ended and the feature plays. That’s a strong barrier and not a guarantee, so turn this on only for discs you trust, and run the server as an unprivileged user. The sandbox in detail.

Java and licensing#

  • Java isn’t shipped. The server and the module contain no Java runtime. Whoever runs the server installs one, under that Java’s own licence, and it runs as a separate program.
  • The runtime is ours. runtime.jar is original code under AGPL-3.0-or-later, with no code from Sun, Oracle or other players. It provides the classes disc applications link against, so that discs can run.
  • It isn’t certified. The runtime hasn’t passed any compatibility test and makes no claim to. It doesn’t call itself Java, and “BD-J” and “Blu-ray” here only describe which discs it plays.
  • Copy protection is untouched. AACS and BD+ are never decrypted or run.

On a small machine#

  • Only the bytes being played are read. Nothing is copied to disk.
  • Settings → DVDs and Blu-rays → Low-power mode for discs uses the cheapest deinterlacer and the fastest encoder setting.
  • A hardware encoder helps most: see Live transcoding.
  • Subtitle pictures are decoded in two-minute windows as the viewer reaches them, so a 20 GB film is never scanned whole.

Diagnosing a disc#

Settings → WittyDiagnoser checks a disc and makes a report to attach, with nothing copyrighted in it: see WittyDiagnoser.

When reporting a problem, attach that report, and never the disc image.

Limits#

  • AACS-encrypted discs are refused, and BD+ is not run.
  • 3D discs play in 2D, and 4K discs are sent as 1080p unless you choose 2160p.
  • Secondary audio and picture-in-picture are not mixed in.
  • BD-J graphics reach the player at up to 12 pictures a second.
  • It has been tested on custom-authored discs. Retail discs use more of the format: BD-J, 3D and Ultra HD HDR are written, and have only been tested on synthetic data or not at all.
  • On FreeBSD and macOS, the module’s helper program runs without a sandbox. The BD-J limits above are separate from that.
  • Later: HD DVDs, Video CDs and Super Video CDs, and raw-sector images of video discs. A loose .evo file from an HD DVD plays as a copy, with the WittyDVD remote and no menus.

For developers#

WittyBluray is a project of its own. Everything the format can do, and what happens with each, is in Blu-ray disc quirks.

Looking inside a disc. The server’s old bluray command has gone. The tool now comes from the WittyBluray project, and needs Go 1.26 or newer:

go install github.com/wittywilla/wittybluray/cmd/wittybluray@latest
wittybluray info     MY_DISC.iso          # titles, program, playlists, clips, streams, 3D and HDR flags
wittybluray playlist MY_DISC.iso 800      # every item, stream and chapter of one playlist
wittybluray menu     MY_DISC.iso 800      # a playlist's menu: pages, buttons and every command
wittybluray walk     MY_DISC.iso          # simulate inserting the disc: the menu's buttons and commands
wittybluray walk     MY_DISC.iso 2 5      # then press buttons 2 and 5
wittybluray render   MY_DISC.iso out/     # the first menu's pages as PNG files
wittybluray bdj      MY_DISC.iso 20 a.png down enter  # BD-J titles; with Java, run them for 20 seconds, pressing those keys

Their output holds no video or artwork, so it’s safe to attach to a bug report. If a BD-J disc falls back to its feature, bdj usually says why.

How it works:

  1. WittyDisc reads the image directly, with no mounting, including the UDF 2.50 metadata partition Blu-rays use.
  2. The disc’s program is parsed: index.bdmv, MovieObject.bdmv, the playlists and the clip information.
  3. A virtual machine runs the disc’s commands, with 4096 general and 128 player status registers.
  4. A navigator follows first play, menus and titles as the commands dictate, including calls, resumes and timers.
  5. For a menu, it reads the interactive graphics stream and renders the page and each button’s highlight. For a film, it reads the presentation graphics stream for subtitles.
  6. On the server, internal/bdsession converts each playlist’s video to HLS with FFmpeg from the clip’s byte ranges, and the player puts the button and subtitle layers on top.

Everything is written from the public Blu-ray structure. The engine runs as a helper program, so it can update without a new server. Refusing encrypted discs stays with the server, on purpose, so a different helper can’t change it.
