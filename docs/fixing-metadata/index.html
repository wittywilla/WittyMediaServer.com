Fixing wrong details

Put right a wrong match, title, poster or episode number, on one title or hundreds. Whatever you fix stays fixed, and every change can be undone.

All of this is for administrators. To see where details come from in the first place, read Where details come from.

Which tool to use#

You want toUse
Fix one title: its name, poster, plot or ratingEdit on its page
Say it’s really a different film or showEdit, then Search and match
Go through everything that looks wrongFix matches
Fix a show whose episodes have the wrong numbersRenumber episodes
Change a genre or tag on many titles at onceEdit together
Teach the server how a folder is namedNaming rules
Make lots of small fixes in a text editor, or work with the server stoppedThe metadata folder
Fix a gameFix match on its page: see Video games

The Edit dialog#

Press Edit on a film, episode or show. One dialog holds everything about it, in sections you can jump between: Match, Details, Artwork, Skipping, Organise and Logos. Delete, Cancel and Save stay in view however long it runs.

Ctrl+S saves, and Escape closes, asking first if something isn’t saved.

Details has the title, sort name, year, director (or who created a show), season and episode, edition, collection, tagline, overview, genres, tags, content rating, score, network, status and studios. Only what fits that kind of title is shown. Typing waits for Save. Choosing a match, uploading a picture or moving a title happens at once, and a bar offers Undo.

Delete takes a title out of its library and leaves its files on disk. It won’t come back on a scan, and can be brought back from Fix matches, under Removed. Nothing here ever deletes a file.

Choosing the right match#

In the Match section:

  1. Type a name, or paste an id or address from TMDB, IMDb, TheTVDB, TVmaze, AniList or Wikidata, and press Search. For example: tt0000123, tmdb:12, https://www.tvmaze.com/shows/56/a-show.
  2. Every source that’s on is asked at once, films and shows alike, so a disc of a series can be matched to the series. Each result says how likely it is out of 100, and Why? says why in plain words.
  3. Choose a result. Nothing changes yet. It shows what would change, field by field, with what’s there now beside what’s coming.
  4. Untick anything you want to keep. Text you typed, a hand-written NFO and pictures beside the file are shown but not ticked, and locked fields can’t be ticked. For a show, it also says how many of your episodes the source knows.
  5. Press Use this match.

A match you choose stays: no scan or refresh changes it, even if the file is replaced by a better copy.

Remove this match takes away what the source gave and keeps what you typed. The title is named after its file again, and nothing is looked up for it until you choose one.

Check for changes shows what looking it up again would change, and keeps the match it has.

Artwork#

Press Artwork on a title’s page, Change on its poster, or Change beside a picture in the edit dialog’s Artwork section. Each opens the artwork picker, a window of its own in the middle of the screen (the whole screen on a phone), with a tab for each kind of picture the title has: poster, backdrop, banner and title logo.

  • The grid has every picture from every source, each with where it came from and its size. The one in use says In use.
  • The preview on the right shows the highlighted picture large, at the shape it’s shown.
  • Upload a file: JPEG, PNG, WebP or GIF, up to 20 MB and 25 megapixels. The server checks it’s a real picture and writes it again from its pixels, so nothing else in the file (such as where a photo was taken) is kept.
  • Paste a picture’s address: a web address, or a picture in one of your libraries, then Preview.
  • Use a frame from the video, for a film or an episode.
  • Make a title poster: the title’s words as a poster, on a colour of its own or over a frame from the video. Change the words, add a second line such as the year, and pick the colour. It can be used for any title, even one that has a poster.

Nothing changes until Apply, which uses every picture you chose, on every tab, at once, and can be undone. Cancel, Escape or Back leaves everything as it was. By keyboard or remote: the arrows move in the grid, Enter previews, and Enter again applies.

The edit dialog also has Remove, and Revert where there’s something to go back to.

A film with no poster gets a frame from its video. Under Settings → Metadata agents → Artwork, choose a poster made from its title instead, or nothing (a plain card). A picture beside the file or from a source always comes first, and replaces one the server made.

Pictures from sources are fetched by your server, so no browser or app contacts them. Without a TMDB key, a film gets the poster in its folder, or else a free one from Wikimedia Commons where Wikidata names one: mostly older and public-domain films, as most posters aren’t free. Each one’s licence and author are listed under Settings → Legal, in Posters from Wikimedia Commons. TV shows get theirs from TVmaze.

With Save information and artwork next to your media on, the chosen poster and backdrop are also copied to the standard names beside the file, and the NFO is rewritten. A hand-written one is kept as .nfo.bak.

Skipping#

On a film or an episode, this section lists the Recap, the Intro, the Credits and the Advert breaks, each with where it starts and ends and where that came from: the file’s chapters, a .skip.json or .edl beside the file, comparing the season’s episodes, the show’s own intro, or set by hand. Type times such as 1:05, add or remove advert breaks, and press Save skip times. What you set wins over everything else and no scan changes it. Leave the end of the credits empty for credits that run to the end.

  • To go back to the file’s times for one part, empty the times you set for it, and save. Back to what the files say does it for all of them.
  • Pick in player opens the video with a bar over it: choose the part, play or seek to the moment, press Starts here or Ends here, then Save. Mark as many parts as you like, and press Done to come back.

These save at once, with their own button, not with the dialog’s Save.

On a show, Where the intro ends sets one intro for every episode with none of its own: type a time, or press Pick in player, play the first episode to the moment, and press the button there. Blank means work it out from each file. To have the server find each episode’s intro and credits by ear, use Find intros and credits on the show’s page: see Finding intros nothing marks.

Save or undo your other changes first, because picking a time leaves the dialog. Discs have their own menus, so none of this is offered for them.

Scenes and skips#

⋯ → Edit scenes and skips on a film’s or an episode’s page (or the player’s ⋯ menu while it plays) edits the title’s .wittyinfo (the format), which can say much more than the section above and wins over it:

  • Skips: any number of parts, each an intro, recap, credits, preview, a scene after the credits, an advert break or any other part you name, with a Skip button, Skip by itself or Only mark it. Drag them on the timeline, or type exact times. This video has no… stops the season’s or show’s defaults and anything found by itself from adding a kind. The faint bands on the timeline, and What plays now, show what is in force and where it came from.
  • Scenes: titles and start times that take the place of the file’s chapters in the player’s Scenes; Copy the video’s chapters starts from those.
  • Content: what some people may not want to see or hear, and how strong it is. Each profile chooses what to do about it.
  • Details, Pictures: a title, overview, genres and the rest, and posters, backdrops, logos or episode pictures kept in the file itself. Each kind can hold several: Add another puts one more after it, a small picture’s Show this one first makes it the one shown, and × takes it out. The title’s picture chooser offers all of them too.
  • File: where it is, which cut it was made for, which version of the format it follows, Export, Import… and Remove.

In the player, Mark start and Mark end set the picked part (or start a new one) at the moment playing; Frame steps a frame and ±1 s moves the part. The player’s own skip button tries the parts as they are, before saving. From a title’s page, Preview saves and opens the player a few seconds before the part.

On a show, ⋯ → Skip defaults for every episode edits show.wittyinfo, or a season’s file with For.

Saving writes the .wittyinfo beside the video when Settings → Metadata → Save information and artwork next to your media and Write .wittyinfo and .wittyaudio files next to your media are both on. Otherwise the server keeps it, and Export downloads it to put there by hand; the server’s copy wins over a file beside the video until you Remove it. A file made for another cut of the video (its length is off by more than a few seconds) isn’t used: the editor says so and offers Use it anyway.

Languages says which language the file is written in and adds translations: each part’s and scene’s name, the title, overview and the words of a song. Everyone sees the language closest to their own, and the file’s own words where nothing is translated. Any language code works, such as de-AT or yue.

Editing is for a desktop: the web app in a browser on a computer, or the desktop app. In TV mode and the TV apps the entries say (desktop only) and explain where to go, because a remote can’t place a time to the frame. What you make on a computer plays the same on the TV.

Songs and albums#

A song’s ⋯ → Lyrics and details and an album’s ⋯ → Album details and cover edit its .wittyaudio (the format), for administrators:

  • Lyrics: the words line by line. Play the song at the top and press Line starts now as each line begins, or type the times; Paste words to time them fills the lines from plain text.
  • Music: artists, album, track and disc, composers, label, ISRC, tempo and key, moods, and loudness: a gain, where to start and stop playing, and a fade at the end.
  • Pictures, Languages and File work as they do for a film. An album’s file holds what every song on it shares; a song’s own file wins over it. The song files themselves are never changed.

Converting a whole library#

Settings → Metadata → Convert a library (and first-time setup’s Details and artwork step) gathers the NFO files, posters, skip files, subtitles and .lrc lyrics already beside your media into .wittyinfo and .wittyaudio files, or writes every one back out as those usual files. With WittyPlay installed, game lists, box art and screenshots go into .wittyplay files the same way. Each kind has its own progress bar; it waits for any scan to finish first and carries on in Tasks if you leave. Rename the old files with .bak on the end keeps them out of the way without deleting anything.

Bonus features, versions and moving#

The Organise section:

  • It’s a bonus feature takes a trailer, featurette, deleted scene, interview or short out of the lists, and puts it on the page of the film or show it belongs to. Make it a title of its own undoes that.
  • This is another version of… does the same for a second copy of a film, such as a DVD and a 4K file. Its page then offers a choice of version. Split it off undoes that.
  • Move to, under In the wrong library?, puts a title in another library that holds the same kind of thing. Its files don’t move: the library it was found in leaves it alone from then on, and the new one looks after it. Where it can’t move, it says why.

Logos#

Anywhere a logo is needed, Change opens the same place. Search by name, and choose among what’s found, with its source and licence beside it and a link to its page. Or use Upload your own, which a search won’t replace unless you choose one, or No logo: just the name. A change applies everywhere that name appears on your server.

To change the logo ofGo to
A studio or network on a titleEdit, then Logos, or Change logo on its own page
A channel from your libraryIts editor, under Logo. You can also give it another name to go by
A tuner or playlist channelSettings → Channels & tuners, the tuner, then Change beside the channel. See Logos and title art
A console or a game studioA game’s page, under Logos
Any of these, or a streaming serviceSettings → Metadata agents, under Logos, then Find or set a logo. A name nothing has yet can be started from there

Downloading logos needs your agreement first, and these places explain that and link to it. Uploading and choosing none work without it. See The logo service.

Refreshing a title#

Refresh on a title’s page shows what looking it up again would change, and you choose what to take. Update it applies that, and Undo is offered straight after. The title keeps its match, and locked fields stay as they are.

If nothing would change, you can press Look again anyway.

The Fix matches page#

Open Fix matches from a library’s page, where a count shows how many need a look, or from Settings → Metadata agents. It covers film, disc, TV and anime libraries, worst first. It has three tabs: Review, Naming rules and History. Choose a library at the top to narrow it down.

Opening it asks nobody anything: how sure each match is comes from what’s stored, comparing the file’s name and year with what it’s matched to.

Under ReviewWhat’s there
Needs a lookNo match, or one under 70%
No match, Not sure, Looks rightThose on their own
You chose, Left aloneWhat you’ve decided, which nothing changes
EverythingThe whole list
RemovedTitles taken out of a library. Bring it back returns one

To fix one:

  1. Choose a title. It finds suggestions, one request per source. If a file’s whole name finds nothing, its first words are dropped and it’s tried again.
  2. Press Accept on the right one, or choose a suggestion to see what would change first. Search takes a name, an id or an address.
  3. If nothing fits, press It has no match. If it’s right but scored low, press It’s right: stop asking. Edit details opens the Edit dialog.

To go faster:

  • Find suggestions for all does the whole list in the background, gently. Stop ends it.
  • Choose the 4 that are 90% sure ticks those at once, and Accept their best suggestions accepts them. The number is however many there are.

A year you typed, or one kept from before, counts when scoring, so an exact name and year comes to 100%. The words beside a score are Very likely (90 or more), Likely (70 or more), Not sure (40 or more) and Unlikely.

The arrow keys and a remote reach everything too. Moving down the list follows along in the panel beside it, and OK on a title goes to its first suggestion.

Fixing episode numbers#

When a show’s episodes have the wrong numbers:

  1. On the Fix matches page, choose the show, and press Renumber episodes.
  2. Each file is shown beside what the source calls that number. Not listed means the source doesn’t have that number.
  3. Change the numbers by hand, or choose a Season and use Move episodes by with Move them or Move seasons instead. Number them in order counts through them from 1.
  4. Press Save.

Numbers you change are locked, so a scan keeps them.

Editing several titles together#

On the Fix matches page, tick some titles, and press Edit together. You can add or take away genres and tags, set a year or a content rating, and lock or unlock. Press Change, which says how many, and Undo all of it is offered straight after.

Naming rules#

An id in a file or folder’s name always wins over a search, and doesn’t appear in the title. The usual forms all work:

My Film (2001) [tmdbid-123].mkv
My Film (2001) {imdb-tt0000123}.mkv
A Show [tvdb-456]
A Show [tvmaze-78]
A Show [anilist-90]

A folder with a naming of its own can have rules, under Fix matches → Naming rules. Choose a library, and press Add a rule. The first rule that fits a file decides.

FieldWhat it does
How the path readsUses {title}, {year}, {edition}, {tmdb}, {imdb}, {tvdb}, {any}, and * for anything within a folder’s name. For example, Harbour Studio/{year} - {title}
Where to lookLook everywhere, one source only, or Don’t look it up
Add tagsTags for what it fits, separated by commas
These are bonus featuresMarks what it fits as a trailer, featurette and so on
OnTurns the rule off without removing it

Try them shows what each file would become, without changing anything. Press Save to keep them. Rules apply to new and changed files when the library is next scanned.

History and undo#

Every match, edit and renumbering can be undone, right after it, or later under Fix matches → History. Press Undo beside an entry. Changes made together, such as one apply from the metadata folder, are one entry and go back together.

The last 5,000 changes are kept, and the page shows the newest 100.

From the command line#

Nothing on this page needs a terminal. When the server won’t start, or you’d rather not use the page, these do related work:

CommandWhat it does
wittymediaserver metadata checkSays what’s wrong with the metadata folder’s files. See On the command line
wittymediaserver metadata apply --applyApplies your edits from that folder
wittymediaserver metadata undo <batch>Takes one apply back
wittymediaserver doctor --only metadataLooks for broken matches, missing artwork, duplicates and unreadable NFO files. See Repair
wittymediaserver doctor remap --from <old> --to <new>Tells the server a library’s folder moved. See Moved a drive?
