Getting started

This guide takes you from nothing to a film playing on your screen. On Windows and on a Mac you won’t need the command line.

What you need#

  • A computer that stays on while you watch. Windows 10 or 11, macOS 11 or newer, Linux, FreeBSD 14 or newer, or a NAS that runs Docker.
  • Your media. Films and TV in nearly any format, music, books, or images of DVDs and Blu-rays you own. Supported formats has the full list. Encrypted discs are refused, with a plain message.
  • A web browser on whatever you watch on, or the WittyMediaClient app.

FFmpeg, the program that converts video, comes with the download. So does the database. There’s nothing else to install.

Install it and start it#

Download the installer for your computer from the Releases page.

Your computerFileWhat to do
Windows.msiOpen it. If SmartScreen warns you, choose More info, then Run anyway. Choose Just me unless it should always be on
Mac.pkgRight-click it, choose Open, then Open again
Debian, Ubuntu.debsudo apt install ./wittymediaserver-….deb
Fedora, RHEL.rpmsudo dnf install ./wittymediaserver-….rpm
FreeBSD.pkgpkg add wittymediaserver-….pkg, then sysrc wittymediaserver_enable=YES and service wittymediaserver start

On Windows and a Mac, open WittyMediaServer from the Start menu or Applications. An icon appears by the clock or in the menu bar, and setup opens in your browser. To get back later, click the icon and choose Open WittyMediaServer, or go to http://localhost:9797.

On Linux and FreeBSD, the server runs as a service. Open http://<this computer>:9797/ in a browser.

With Docker:

docker run -d --name wittymediaserver --restart unless-stopped \
  -p 9797:9797 \
  -v wms-config:/config \
  -v /path/to/movies:/media/movies:ro \
  ghcr.io/wittywilla/wittymediaserver:alpha

This is an alpha. What has been tested says how far each installer has been run. Installing has every download, services, Docker and a NAS.

What first-time setup asks#

Setup happens in a browser. It has twelve steps, listed beside the page. Only the account is required: every other step has Skip for now, and says where to change it later. Answers are saved as you go, so you can stop and carry on another day. First-time setup, step by step has a picture of every page.

  1. Language. The language of the pages, disc menus and sound tracks, and the server’s time zone.
  2. Welcome. Set up a new server, or restore one from a backup.
  3. Your account. A name and a password of at least 8 characters. This account looks after the server. Keep the password safe: forgetting it is hard to undo.
  4. This server. Where its data is kept (on a computer you use), its name and colours, and its address at home.
  5. Modules. Movies, TV and music work straight away. Tick what else you want: discs, games, photos, books, live TV. They download, signed, from the official list. Then choose what happens when a module has a new version.
  6. Libraries. Whether details are saved beside your files, then a kind of media and its folder, as many times as you like.
  7. Set up your modules. Live TV (with a demo tuner to try), Games and Discs, if you added them. Otherwise it’s dimmed and passed over.
  8. Looks. The layout, the colours, and with live TV, the TV guide’s look.
  9. People. Profiles for the family, and a look or theme you require of someone.
  10. Away from home. Peer-to-peer connections, with a test, and how to add an app.
  11. Extras. Posters and keys, logos, lyrics, karaoke voices and streaming lists. All optional.
  12. Ready. What you chose, the address for other devices, and Start watching.

With a remote or the keyboard. The arrows reach everything, and OK presses it. Back goes back a page.

If it asks for a setup code#

On the computer running the server, setup doesn’t ask for one. From any other device it does, so nobody else on your network can claim the server first. The code is on the server’s console when it starts (docker logs wittymediaserver, or journalctl -u wittymediaserver on Linux), and in setup-code.txt in the data folder. See Setting up from another device.

Finding your way around#

What you see depends on the look you chose. Some looks have a bar along the top, some a menu down the side, and some both. Each look works on a TV, a phone, a tablet and a computer.

  • The menu holds Home, your libraries, Search and the modules’ pages. An administrator chooses what’s in it under Settings → Pages & menu. See Pages and the menu.
  • Your picture at the top right opens your account menu: Settings, Help, Sign in a TV, TV mode, Switch profile and Sign out. On a TV or a phone, the same things are under You.
  • The activity button at the top shows scans, conversions and other work in progress. See Background tasks.

To change the look or theme for yourself, open Settings → Appearance. An administrator can require one for everyone. See Themes.

Put your files in order#

The server finds videos in any folder layout, but a tidy one gets the right titles and posters. Keep films and TV in separate folders. Put the year after a film’s title. Number episodes like S02E01:

Movies/
  The Long Harbour (2001)/The Long Harbour (2001).mkv
  Paper Lanterns (2014).mp4
TV Shows/
  Night Ferry/
    Season 02/
      Night Ferry - S02E01 - The Crossing.mkv

Libraries covers every kind of media.

Add your first library#

A library is one or more folders of one kind of media. Setup offers this. Later, use Settings → Libraries → Add library.

  1. Choose the kind of media, such as Movies.
  2. Change its name if you like.
  3. Choose the folder:
    • On the computer running the server, Choose folder… opens the computer’s own folder window.
    • Browse folders shows the server’s drives in the page, from any device. Open the folder and press Use this folder. This needs the folder browser module, which setup ticks for you.
    • Or type the folder’s full path.
  4. Press the button that adds it, such as Add Movies.

Scanning starts in the background. Titles appear as they’re found, and posters and plots follow. The activity button shows how it’s going.

Play your first film#

  1. Open Home, or your library from the menu, and choose a poster.
  2. Press Play. If you’ve watched some of it, you’ll see Resume from and the time, with Play from start beside it.

For a disc image, Play starts it like a disc player, with its real menu. See DVDs and Blu-rays.

KeyWhat it does
SpacePause and play
← →Back or forward 10 seconds
↑ ↓Volume
fFull screen
mMute

A TV remote or a game controller works too. Watching and listening has everything the player does. If it doesn’t play, see Troubleshooting.

Posters and descriptions#

Films and shows get details with no key. For better matches and more artwork, add a free TMDB or TheTVDB key under Settings → Metadata agents, then press Refresh on a title. If a title is wrong, press Edit on its page, or use Fix matches. See Where details come from.

Watch on other devices#

At home. Any phone, tablet, computer or TV browser on your home network can open the server.

  1. Find the address. It’s on the last page of setup, and in Settings → About. It looks like http://192.168.1.20:9797.
  2. Type it into the browser on the other device.
  3. If it doesn’t load, the server’s firewall may be in the way. See Troubleshooting.

The app. WittyMediaClient is for Windows, macOS, Linux, FreeBSD, Android, Android TV, iPhone, iPad and Apple TV. It finds your server at home by itself. Sign it in with a code from Sign in a TV in your account menu. See Apps and devices.

From away. Your server answers only at home until you choose a way in. The easiest is From away: turn it on in Settings → Remote Access, and a device joins with a code, with nothing to set up on your router. See Remote access.

Add the family#

Under Settings → Profiles, add an Adult, Kids or Guest profile for each person. Everyone keeps their own progress. A Kids profile only shows titles up to the age you choose. See Accounts and profiles and Profiles and parental controls.

Words you’ll see#

WordWhat it means
LibraryOne or more folders of one kind of media
ScanThe server looking through a library’s folders for new or changed files
LookThe layout: where the menu sits and how pages are arranged
ThemeThe colours
ModuleA part you can add or remove, such as disc menus or games. Each is its own project, with its own updates
Direct playThe file is sent as it is, with nothing converted
TranscodingConverting a video as it plays, so a device can play it
Disc imageOne file holding a whole disc, usually an .iso
NFOA small text file beside a video, with its title, plot and year

Alpha software: it changes quickly and can fail or lose data. Keep backups, including your data folder, before you upgrade. No media comes with WittyMediaServer. Copy-protected discs, encrypted broadcasts and encrypted game files are refused with a plain message. Nothing here unlocks them.

This site has no cookies, trackers or analytics. Your choice of colours is kept in your own browser. How this site handles privacy. Change the colours.
