The small print

No catch. We checked. Twice.

Some media projects hide the catch in the small print, on the pricing page, in paragraph four of a cheerful blog post, or somewhere in a long social media thread. We put ours on the home page. There isn’t one.

  • There will never be a WittyMediaServer Pro

    No Pro. No Plus. No Premium, Ultimate or Founders’ Edition. Nobody here is quietly switching features off in the free version so a paid one looks better. If the server can do it, you can do it, today and every day after.

  • Our own work, with receipts

    Every line of WittyMediaServer’s own code was written for WittyMediaServer, and its whole history is public: every change, with its date, its author and what came before it. When you know how to build something better, you don’t need to borrow anyone’s homework. And when anyone wonders, the answer is a link, not a social media post.

    Every change, from the first commit

  • Nothing behind a paywall

    Every feature is in the download, for everyone. No “unlock this for a small monthly fee”, no greyed-out buttons, and no subscription lurking behind a “coming soon”. Your media, your server, your features. Wild concept.

  • No waitlist, no velvet rope

    It’s an alpha, and anyone can download it right now. No invite codes, no sign-up form, no queue, and no being made to feel special for the privilege of testing someone’s software for free.

  • Open since the first commit

    Public under Apache-2.0 from day one. Not “open source soon”. Not “source available”. Not “open once we’ve worked out how to charge for it”. All of it, from the first line.

    Read every line

  • Built in the open, tools included

    Every library and tool it’s made with is listed, with its licence and the people who wrote it. Standing on the shoulders of giants is fine. Pretending the shoulders are yours isn’t.

    Every third-party licence

  • Licences and credits, actually done

    Every screenshot shows only openly licensed or public-domain media, and every work in it is credited with its licence. It turns out a media server can look great without borrowing anyone’s film posters. Who knew.

    Every picture’s credits

  • No borrowed fame

    No studio logos, no streaming-service artwork, and no winking hints at partnerships with media giants who’ve never heard of us. We aren’t “trusted by” anyone we haven’t met. We spend our evenings writing code, not threads. Just a media server, made by people who use one.

  • Remote access is free. All of it. Forever.

    Watching away from home costs nothing, for every feature, and always will. No subscription to stream your own films to your own phone, no remote add-on, and nothing that works on your Wi-Fi only until you pay. It’s your server. You already paid for the electricity.

Everything in one place

One server for everything you keep

Point it at your folders. It finds what’s there, fetches the pictures and details, and keeps your place across every screen. Discs, games, photos, books and TV tuners are modules you add from the server’s store. The server works without any of them.

Films and TV

Posters, plots and ratings, Continue watching, skip intro and instant seeking. No API key needed.

DVDs and Blu-rays

Disc images play like a disc player: intros, animated menus, scene selection, extras and the disc’s own subtitles.

Live TV and channels

Channels made from your library in one press, a guide, tuners, IPTV and recording.

Music and audiobooks

Albums and artists, lyrics and karaoke, radio, podcasts, and books that remember your place.

Books and comics

EPUB, PDF, comics and manga, with highlights, notes and a pen.

Photos and videos

A timeline, albums and an offline map, a feed of vertical videos, and creators’ videos by channel.

Games

Your own game files, played in a browser with a gamepad, keyboard or touch.

Everyone at home

Who’s watching? profiles with their own progress, and Kids profiles that only show titles for their age.

Discs, done properly

Real DVD and Blu-ray menus, in any browser

Keep your discs as images and play them the way a player would, on a phone, a laptop or the TV.

  • Intros, animated menus, scene selection and extras
  • The disc’s own audio tracks, subtitles and angles
  • DVDs, and Blu-rays including 4K and HDR
  • Copy-protected discs are refused with a plain message. Nothing unlocks them

Two parts, one setup

The server, and the apps that reach it

The server holds your library, and any web browser at home can use it. The apps are optional.

The server

WittyMediaServer

Self-hosted software that keeps your films, shows, discs, music, books and games on a computer you own, and streams them to every screen in your home. It plays DVDs and Blu-rays with their real menus, in any browser.

Install it on the computer that holds your media. Every other screen reaches it through a web browser, with no app needed.

About the server Browse modules
The apps

WittyMediaClient

Apps for TV boxes, phones, tablets and desktops that connect to your WittyMediaServer, for the devices where a browser isn’t the right fit.

Optional. The server’s whole interface already runs in any web browser.

About the apps Get the apps

There’s also WittyMediaPlayer, for discs and files, and WittyReader, for books, PDFs and comics. Neither needs a server, and neither connects to one: they’re for files on the computer in front of you. See every project.

Modules

Add only what you need

Modules add what your server can do, such as DVD menus, streamed games or live TV. Most are projects of their own, signed, and added from Settings → Modules while the server keeps running.

Yours

It runs on your computer, for your home

No accounts with anyone, no telemetry and no cloud. You add only media you have the right to use: see the legal notes.

Your computer

Your library stays on hardware you own. Other screens reach it over your own network.

Nothing reported

No telemetry. It goes online for posters and details and to look for updates, and Settings → Legal → Privacy lists every outside service and what turns it off.

Signed modules

Every module version is signed, checked before it runs, and removable without touching your media.

Open source

The server is licensed under Apache-2.0, and every project’s source is on GitHub.

Try it on a computer you already have

Install the server, open its page in a browser, and point it at a folder of films. Setup takes a few minutes.
