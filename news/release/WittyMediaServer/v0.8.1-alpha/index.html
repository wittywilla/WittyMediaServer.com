WittyMediaServer 0.8.1-alpha

Release notes

What’s new in 0.8.1-alpha

Released 2026-11-23.

  • Live TV’s channel banner shows for 3 seconds (#556). Choosing a channel shows its name and what’s on for three seconds instead of seven, then gets out of the way.
  • Albums on more than one disc, in order, with their covers (#558). A 2-CD album whose files have no disc tag listed its songs 1, 1, 2, 2, 3, 3… across both discs, and a TV showed only the first two. The disc is now read from the name when the tags don’t say (201-artist-title.flac is disc 2, track 1; so is 2-01 Title.flac or a song in a CD2 folder), and every app heads each disc’s songs Disc 1, Disc 2, numbered from 1 again. A release’s own picture (000-artist-album.jpg, Album (Front).jpg) is now the album’s cover when there’s no cover.jpg or folder.jpg, as long as the picture is whole (a cut-short one leaves the art inside the songs). Both apply with an ordinary Scan for changes: the files aren’t read again. A picture the server can’t make smaller (some release groups’ JPEGs) is sent whole instead of “no artwork”.
  • A TV’s album page, rebuilt (#558). The cover (or a tile with the album’s name when there’s none) with the title, artist, year, songs, length and genre beside it; Play, Shuffle and More (Add to queue, Go to artist, Share); the songs right under them, a guest’s name under its song (“Lady Gaga Feat. Beyoncé”), a playing mark in place of the playing song’s number, and the remote scrolling the list. The keys along the bottom are one short line. For the apps: a DetailHeader’s menuAfter, the shuffle, queue and artist actions, a TrackList’s discs and a Track’s disc and credit (docs/SDUI.md). A playlist of songs shows square covers, not posters cut to a portrait.
  • TV mode shows no keyboard keys (#558). The bar of keys along the foot of every TV page (“Enter Select · Esc Back · Ctrl+[ …”, then the remote’s after a key was pressed) is one Keys button now, which opens the remote’s or the controller’s keys in a dialog (Back closes it). Now playing and karaoke drop their keyboard footers on a TV, More info drops its “I”, and the player’s Keyboard shortcuts is Keys. Every key still works; a computer out of TV mode shows them as before. npm run test:tv-keys checks every TV page at 1920×1080 and 1280×720.
  • TV mode’s guide fits a TV. At 1080p and 720p the guide shows at least five whole channels, never one cut in half, and moves a whole row at a time; the preview starts smaller and the programme’s details take less room. The bar of guide buttons and keys along the foot is behind a Shortcuts button beside the channel set (closed at first, remembered on the device; the remote’s Menu opens it and Back closes it; letters and colour buttons work either way), and a Help button lists what the remote does and the full legal words, which otherwise sit on one line. As in the Android TV app (WittyMediaClient #84).
  • Readable titles and text on every theme (#444). Words over a picture (Home’s billboard, a title’s page) now sit on a scrim in the theme’s own page colour, so a light theme puts dark words on a light wash and a dark one the reverse, and a test checks the drawn page’s contrast on every bundled theme. The web app’s icons (favicon, home-screen and install icons) are redrawn in the themes’ new colours, so a browser or phone that kept the old icon shows the new one once it fetches it again.
  • TV mode keeps to what a remote does well (#469). Editors with many fields, typing, files or drag handles (libraries, pages and menu, channels and bumpers, transcoding, network, remote access, logs, modules, backups, the theme maker, the layout editor and more) show a card in TV mode instead, with what is set now. On a TV device (the TV apps, Plasma Bigscreen, a TV’s own browser) it says to use the WittyMediaClient app on a Windows or Mac computer, or a web browser, with the page’s address and a QR code; on a computer in TV mode it says to leave TV mode, and its Leave TV mode button opens the same page in the normal layout, with Back to TV mode there. Playback, profiles, security and sharing show their values without changing them. Browsing, playing, Live TV, WittyTV, karaoke, music and every console and controller setting stay as they were. One table (pkg/sdui/data/tvlevels.json, in the SDUI catalogue as tvLevels) decides it for the web app and the TV apps; pages, module settings pages and their rows can say tv themselves. GET /api/sdui/tv-summary/{id} gives the card its lines. Settings on a television opens on its menu. See docs/TV-MODE.md.
  • The card for a computer’s settings is centred in TV mode, as the sign-in screens are: the screen’s name, one line, the QR code large with the address under it, what is set now, and Back focused. The Android TV and Apple TV apps draw the same card.
  • Live TV check-up (#472, with WittyTuner 0.5.0):
    • Recording an IPTV channel survives a dropped stream. A stream that ended early ended the recording, sometimes marked done with half a programme. It’s now opened again and added to the same file until the programme’s end, and the finished recording says how many times it dropped. HLS channels with WebVTT subtitles or data tracks no longer fail to record.
    • Watching survives a blip. When a channel’s stream stops and the first try to open it again fails (a provider’s brief refusal), the server tries again after 1, 2, 4 and 8 seconds before telling viewers it stopped.
    • Errors in words. A failed live stream said FFmpeg’s own output; it now says what happened (the stream isn’t there any more, refused this server, the provider had a problem, couldn’t be reached) and what to try. The detail stays in the log.
    • Guide matching sees every name a guide gives a channel (numbers and call signs are often among them) and its icon (wittytv migration 10), and the host API’s GET /tv/guide/ids says each one’s source and how far ahead its listings go.
    • tvg-shift: a channel’s guide is moved by its playlist’s shift, so a “+1” channel shows its programmes an hour later.
    • A guide read twice at once no longer leaves the guide empty: one source’s guide is read one at a time.
    • New page: Live TV: tuners, IPTV and the guide, with the demo tuner, big playlists, guide matching and what each error means.
  • Scenes and skips: the .wittyinfo file and its editor (#459). A .wittyinfo beside a film or an episode (or show.wittyinfo / seasonNN.wittyinfo for every episode) holds what people add by hand: skip segments (intro, recap, credits, preview, a scene after the credits, advert breaks and any other part) each with a skip button, skipping by itself or only a mark; scenes in place of the file’s chapters; content filters each profile chooses to skip, mute or be warned about; replacement details, pictures, subtitles and playing preferences; who made it; and the cut it was made for, so times for another cut are flagged rather than used. It is a small ZIP archive (or a bare JSON manifest written by hand), read strictly with size, count and name limits, and nothing in it is ever fetched or run. A video’s own file beats everything else; a season’s or show’s beats chapters and what was found by itself. The editor is on a title’s ⋯ menu (Edit scenes and skips), a show’s (Skip defaults for every episode) and in the player’s ⋯ menu, with Mark start / Mark end at the moment playing, frame steps and nudges, a timeline of draggable ranges, exact times, and a preview that plays the player’s own skip button. Edits are written beside the media when Write .wittyinfo and .wittyaudio files next to your media and Save information… next to your media are on, and kept by the server otherwise, with Import and Export. Settings, Playback gains Skip previews, Skip other marked parts, Do as the video’s file says and Content. The new wittyinfo program makes, checks, packs, unpacks and edits files without a server (wittyinfo edit opens the same editor on this computer). See the format; API /api/wittyinfo/... and segments in markers and playback answers (client API).
  • Songs and albums: the .wittyaudio file (#459). The same archive for music, named after the music plugin: a song’s (or album.wittyaudio for every song on an album) artists, album, track and disc numbers, composers, label, ISRC, tempo, key, moods, a biography, words timed line by line (and word by word), loudness, a start and end trim and a fade. A song’s ⋯ → Lyrics and details and an album’s ⋯ → Album details and cover open it, with Line starts now to time the words while the song plays. See the format.
  • Every language, in the files and the editor (#459). A .wittyinfo or .wittyaudio says what language it is written in and carries translations of each name, title, the details and the words, keyed by language tag; everyone sees the closest to their own. Audio and subtitle choices in a file take any language code, not just the common ones, and the editor, its settings and the setup step are translated into all 26 languages the apps are.
  • Convert a library to these files and back (#459). First-time setup’s Details and artwork step and Settings → Metadata → Convert a library gather the NFO files, posters, skip files, subtitles and .lrc lyrics already beside your media into .wittyinfo and .wittyaudio files (and, with WittyPlay, game details into .wittyplay), or write them back out as those usual files, with a progress bar for each. Setup’s Continue waits until a conversion it started has finished; old files can be kept with .bak on the end, and nothing is deleted.
  • Several pictures of each kind, and a version in every file (#459). A .wittyinfo or .wittyaudio can hold a list of posters, backdrops, album covers, back covers and the rest, the first one shown: the editor’s Pictures adds another, shows a different one first or takes one out, and a title’s picture chooser offers every one. Converting a library gathers numbered extras (Film-poster-2.jpg, fanart2.jpg, an extrafanart folder, cover-2.jpg, back.jpg, cd.jpg) and writes them back out. Every file says which version of the format it follows ("version": 1); a newer file is read for what is known and the rest kept, and says so (Versions). Pictures in a .wittyinfo also show in the editor again: their address now takes the sign-in a picture needs.
  • The editor is for a desktop (#459). On a TV, Edit scenes and skips and the music entries say they are desktop only and where to go instead: a remote and a TV’s hardware can’t place times precisely enough. Files made on a computer play the same on the TV.
  • Peer-to-peer connections, made sturdier and easier (#476, #460). The page now opens with one plain answer, such as Direct connection works or Your network blocks direct connections; we’ll use a relay when WittyRelay is available, with what to do, and Test my connection gives the same answer. Your own WittyRelay: point the server at a WittyRelay you run in Docker (a new project, wittywilla/WittyRelay), with Check showing what the relay allows before you use it. Every relay that carries whole connections now says its limits (speed, picture size, data and time) before use, the apps show them, and films sent through a relay are made to fit them. Help other servers lets you set the upload you share and the picture size. Fixed: an app could wait up to five minutes to reconnect after the server came back (libp2p’s own dial back-off on top of ours); the first request after a network change could fail; the first hole punch was nearly always wasted, so a connection stayed on a relay for a minute or more (now about ten seconds in the simulated networks); a relay you named waited for AutoNAT before it was used. Invites and codes are limited to 10 per account in 10 minutes. Tests: fuzzing of the framing, invite links and relay addresses, many devices at once, and a WSL network simulator (scripts/p2p-netsim) for NAT types, a shared address, no UDP, loss and delay, a dropped link and a changed address.
  • The app speaks the server’s language. Every page, for everyone, follows the language chosen in setup or Settings: 26 languages, with Portuguese as written in Brazil or in Portugal, and Arabic and Hebrew laid out right to left. Setup’s Welcome screen has Back, to fix a wrong language or time zone. Modules get the language through the host API. (#387) The text added since (TV mode’s cards, live TV and IPTV, peer-to-peer and your own WittyRelay, karaoke on DirectML, the new setup and the data folder’s messages) is translated too.
  • First-time setup asks for the language and time zone first, on a screen before the welcome page. They’re saved as soon as the account is made, and the Name and address step shows them again to change. (#385)
  • Added: first-time setup asks where to keep the settings and data, on a desktop install (the Windows tray app, the Mac app, a Linux desktop): Documents (the default), the install folder when the server can write there, or a folder you choose, typed or browsed to. It shows free space, checks the folder can be written, and warns when OneDrive, iCloud Drive or another sync program keeps the folder, offering a local one. The choice is kept in data-location.json in the account’s config folder, which the server, the tray app, the installer and the uninstaller read; a folder that isn’t there (a drive not connected) stops the start with a message instead of making a new, empty server. Settings → Data folder moves it later: the server restarts, copies, checks the copy, switches, and keeps the old copy until you delete it there. Existing installs keep their folder. The data folder gets a README.txt saying what each part is; docs/DATA-FOLDER.md says it at length. GET/POST /api/data-location, POST /api/data-location/check, DELETE /api/data-location/request, POST /api/data-location/old/delete, GET /api/data-location/progress. (#471)
  • Fixed: first-time setup’s Live TV and Games steps were an empty card once their module was installed. The app asked what modules add to it only once, before there was an account, and never again; it now asks again after a refusal, with the server’s state, and after the Modules step installs something. A module’s step that can’t load says so instead of drawing nothing. (#378)
  • Fixed: first-time setup’s Choose colours step ticked Witty after another look was chosen, while the page wore that look’s colours. It now ticks the theme the chosen look defines until someone picks another, and “Start with the look’s own theme” goes back to it. (#380)
  • Fixed: first-time setup’s Modules step showed generic glyphs, not each module’s own icon. The icons now ship inside the server (web/public/module-icons, drawn by scripts/genicon -target server from the catalogue), so setup shows the same pictures as the catalogue without fetching anything from the internet. (#382)
  • Fixed: first-time setup’s Live TV and Games steps were an empty card once their module was installed. The app asked what modules add to it only once, before there was an account, and never again; it now asks again after a refusal, with the server’s state, and after the Modules step installs something. A module’s step that can’t load says so instead of drawing nothing. (#378)
  • Fixed: after first-time setup, Home still said “Finish setting up WittyTuner” (or WittyPlay) although the Live TV or Games step had been gone through. The step a module draws in setup never told the server; the wizard now records it when someone moves on from it, as done or put off (POST /api/setup/module-steps/skip takes done). A module’s step that crashes shows the “set it up later” note, and setup carries on. (#427)
  • First-time setup asks for the language and time zone first, on a screen before the welcome page. They’re saved as soon as the account is made, and the Name and address step shows them again to change. (#385)
  • Settings → Modules and the module store show each module’s picture on a server that hasn’t read the module catalogue yet, instead of a drawn letter: the pictures in the list a server is built with are fetched from beside the official catalogue, not from a bare path that was refused. A picture that can’t be fetched is logged as a warning (cat=modules). (#384)
  • Added: Voice settings in karaoke, a panel of controls for the singer’s part and the music under it: low cut, warmth, presence, air, compression, pan, doubling, echo (amount, time, repeats), reverb (amount, room size), and the music’s bass, treble and a dip that makes room for the voice, with presets (Clear voice, Studio, Concert hall, Stadium, Slapback echo, Old radio, Duet spread). Kept for each account on the device. With the quick version, the voice is the song less the music (#424).
  • Changed: karaoke’s Mix is three sliders, Voice, Background Music (every part but the singer, together) and Track (the whole song), in place of a slider per separated part. Controls at the end of the bar hides any of them, and Key and the words’ timing; what’s hidden is kept in me/ui musicPlayer.karHide. The rules for apps are in docs/CLIENT-API.md, “Karaoke that sets itself up” (#414).
  • Added: Album art beside the words in karaoke, a setting kept with the music player’s others (me/ui musicPlayer.karArt, off unless chosen). The cover sits at the left of the words on a computer or a TV, and above them, smaller, on a phone held upright (#408).
  • Removed: karaoke’s old in-browser voice control (Voice on / lower / lowest, which cancelled the middle of the stereo mix) and the audio nodes and mono check behind it. Karaoke only ever shows the Mix, which plays the server’s karaoke version; while that’s being made, the Mix waits in its place and says how far along it is, so the old control no longer flashes up first (#407).
  • Fixed: the player’s Scenes was an empty strip behind the seek bar. The looks’ background picture used the class scene, which pinned every scene card behind the page; it’s look-scene now. The scene cards’ own styles, which went with the old disc page in Design 2, are back, so each scene shows its picture and time and “Now playing” sits on its own scene. (#390)
  • Added: Demucs for karaoke, installed with one press in Settings → Lyrics and music information, so karaoke’s properly separated version is there by default. uv (checked against a pinned SHA-256), Python 3.11, Demucs 4.0.1 and CPU-only PyTorch at pinned versions, and the htdemucs model go into the data folder’s separator/ (about 1.1 GB, left out of backups); Remove takes it out. Everything installed is credited with its license text in Settings → About. GET/DELETE /api/karaoke/separator, POST /api/karaoke/separator/install. It runs on the graphics card by default (CUDA on NVIDIA, ROCm on AMD under Linux, Metal on Apple silicon), and any song that fails there is separated again on the processor; ?accel=cpu installs for the processor only. Separate the whole library ahead of time (karaokeLibrary: off by default, or idle) does every song in the background, one at a time and only while the server is idle, stopping at 80% of the karaoke space. Without a separator, GET /media/{id}/karaoke says separator: "installable" so the player can say a cleaner version is an optional install. Machines with under about 4 GB of memory aren’t offered it. Not offered on FreeBSD, Windows on ARM, musl Linux or tiny machines.
  • Added: Demucs on Intel and AMD graphics cards under Windows, through DirectML (#430). On 64-bit Windows the one-press install also brings ONNX Runtime’s DirectML build and onnx (pinned wheels, each checked against a SHA-256 the server carries), converts the htdemucs model to ONNX on the machine (never downloaded converted, from us or anyone), checks it against Demucs on the processor and runs a short test on the card. The order is CUDA, ROCm or Metal first, then DirectML, then the processor; a song that fails on a card is redone on the processor. Runs on in Settings → Lyrics and music information changes it (karaokeDevice: auto, or cuda, rocm, mps, dml, cpu where installed). The Licenses list now names the model’s weights, where they came from and their license. A 3:48 song on an RTX 3070: CUDA 10 s, DirectML 9 s, the processor 65 s.
  • Fixed: the Demucs install no longer puts a python3.11 in the user’s own programs folder or registers Python in the Windows registry; Python stays in the server’s separator/ folder.
  • Fixed: a scan never sits at “12,709 of 12,709 files” while it goes on (WittyPlay #88). The scan line names what a module is working on (“Scanning Super Nintendo: 11,776 of 12,709 files”), then each later step in its own words (“Finding out about what’s new: 100 of 2,293”), and a module’s pages are drawn again while a library it scans is being scanned, so WittyPlay’s consoles show as the scan sorts them.
