Release notes

What’s new in 0.8.6-alpha

Released 2026-11-25.

A fix release for 0.8.5, mostly for a NAS or other small server in Docker: HDR films start again, regular playback no longer opens on black with the sound behind, WittyTV tunes in without a frozen picture, and Settings keeps its menu. Use client 0.6.4 with it. Docker users: as in 0.8.5, publish port 9796 (TCP and UDP) and pass /dev/dri for an Intel or AMD graphics chip; Settings → Live transcoding → Graphics says what the server can use.

  • WittyTV: no sound before the picture at tune-in. A channel’s sound now starts with its first picture, and “Tuning…” stays until there is one, rather than giving way to a frame standing still while the sound plays. The sound comes back after a few seconds at most whatever happens, and a page playing in the background isn’t held.
  • HDR films start again on a NAS without a graphics chip. 0.8.5’s image carries a Vulkan driver that runs on the processor (for games), and HDR films were tone-mapped through it: several times slower, and in a 2 GB container FFmpeg ran out of memory on the first segment, so the picture stayed black and the film never started. That driver isn’t taken for a graphics chip any more: HDR is tone-mapped on the processor as in 0.8.4, and on a real Intel, AMD or NVIDIA chip as before. CI now starts open-GOP, HEVC 10-bit, late-sound and HDR10 test films from the start, part way and after a skip, and fails on a black or frozen picture or the sound out of step.
  • Sound and picture closer together after a change of speed. Watching at 4× and going back to normal could leave the sound out of step with the picture until the next seek. After each real change of speed (half a step or more) the player now lines them up by itself, in the browser, the desktop app, and the Android and Apple apps (client 0.6.4). Played straight from the file it’s back within 20 ms. A video the server converts on a slow machine can still end up to about 60 ms apart after 4×; that part follows in the next release, and CI reports it meanwhile.
  • A NAS that can’t keep up at a speed plays at the fastest speed it can, and says so (web and Android), instead of stalling: it steps down to a smaller picture once, then caps the speed menu for that video.

Settings keeps its menu, and every page reads from one edge (#763)

  • Every Settings choice opens inside Settings, with the menu beside it. Sign in a TV, Connect a device, Your year, Collections and playlists, Fix matches, Controllers, Help and Legal used to open as pages of their own with no menu. They now open beside the menu, under Settings’ own title. Libraries opens Settings’ own Libraries page. Opened any other way, each is the page it always was.
  • The menu no longer disappears on a laptop or a tablet. At 1280 pixels wide and narrower, Settings showed the page alone, without its menu. The menu now stays beside the page down to a tablet’s width. On a phone, Settings is the menu, then the page, with Back to the menu.
  • A module’s settings stay in Settings. Games & emulators threw you into WittyPlay’s console. It is now a Settings page with a button that opens the console. A module’s own page that the menu opens is drawn inside Settings.
  • Settings pages no longer shift sideways. Each page’s title and content started 5 pixels to one side or the other, depending on whether the page was long enough to scroll. Every Settings page now starts at the same place.
  • Module settings for WittyPlay opens again. A group of settings without a title stopped the page with “This page ran into a problem”.

Developer notes:

  • pages/settings/open.ts: inSettings() gives a menu row’s address inside Settings (/settings/open/<address>, or the web’s own Settings page for it). App.tsx PageRoutes draws /settings/open/*, and a module’s app route under /settings/, inside SettingsPage’s frame (its new page prop). InSettings tells a page it is there: SpecPage and Connect a device use PageHead.
  • test-layout.mjs:
    • It takes its pages from the links the menu draws, not the raw paths in settingsmenu.json.
    • A new kind, no-menu, fails a settings page without the menu beside it (Back to it on a phone), or one that opens outside Settings. Before, a page that left Settings was skipped.
    • align covers titles a module draws itself and fails a centred title. It also fails a settings page whose title starts somewhere other than the others at the same size.
  • WittyTV: a quicker tune-in on a small server. On a machine with up to four processors or 4 GB (a NAS, a container held to 2 CPUs), a live picture the processor converts now uses the fastest encoder setting, at most 1080 lines, in 2-second segments, and a stream is ready after its first one. At 2 CPUs a 1080p HEVC 10-bit channel was converted slower than it plays, so the picture stood still at every tune-in; it now keeps up at 1.7 times, and the server has a stream ready in 2.3 to 3.5 s for an HDR10 channel (was 6 to 11 s), 1.6 to 2.1 s for HEVC 10-bit and 1.4 to 1.9 s for H.264. Larger machines and graphics chips are as before.
  • The processor’s graphics drivers are called what they are. Since 0.8.5 the image has Mesa’s software drivers (llvmpipe for OpenGL, lavapipe for Vulkan), which WittyPlay uses to draw GameCube and Wii with no graphics card. The Graphics status (Settings → Live transcoding, wittymediaserver hardware, the log) now says “Software (no graphics chip)” for them, never a yes, including on a chip of another make that only they could serve. Nothing in transcoding uses Vulkan but HDR tone mapping, which refuses a software device; a test keeps it so (no Vulkan encoder, decode pipeline or named decoder; FFmpeg’s automatic decoder choice only alongside NVENC). The no-card CI run checks both.
  • Recent can be clicked into again (WittyPlay). Moving the mouse across Recent’s games kept changing which one was chosen, and the chosen one grows, so the game under the pointer moved and a click landed on another, or opened it at once. The fix for this (#547, for every row with a details panel or a growing card) was undone by a later merge (#558). It’s back, as one rule in web/src/ui/pointerChoose.ts that every such row uses: a pointer only points, a click chooses, and a second click, a double-click or Enter opens; arrows, a remote and a pad move the choice as before. The chosen card is marked aria-current. A unit test fails if a row chooses on hover again, and npm run test:pointer checks Recent, Consoles and All games in a real browser (mouse across, click, click again, focus and Enter).
  • Live TV: +30 is back, up to live. At the owner’s request, the jump forward returns to the live player (#688 had removed it). +30, the timeline, Right, Fast forward and Next (outside an ad break) go ahead no further than the live edge, and +30 is greyed out there. Back to live stays.
  • WittyTV: leaving full screen keeps the channel where it was. Back in the guide, the first row was highlighted for a moment before the channel just left; the little TV took that, dropped the channel playing and tuned it again a second later, at another point in it (and anyone who had paused or gone back lost their place). The guide now keeps the channel just left until the highlight reaches its row. npm run test:tv-handover checks the round trip.
  • WittyTV: a picture that stands still while the sound plays on is put right. The web player (and the apps that show it) watches the pictures it presents. When the clock runs on for a second with no new one, as when a decoder can’t start at a tune-in or a programme join, the sound is silenced and the player seeks to where it is, which starts sound and picture again together; the sound comes back with the picture. A few tries a stream at most, and never for a hidden page playing sound alone.
  • WittyTV: no frozen picture at tune-in on open-GOP H.264 (#760). Some H.264 encodes, many Blu-ray ones among them, mark I-frames that aren’t IDR frames as keyframes, with one IDR at the very start. A channel copied them as they were, so a player tuning in, or a programme joined part way through, had no frame its decoder could start from. Graphics-chip decoders, as most browsers, phones and TVs use, held the picture while the sound played, then showed it late. The server now looks at one keyframe of each such file and converts its picture when the keyframe isn’t an IDR.

Live TV in the phone app (#765)

  • Nothing behind the app’s bars. “Customise this page” under Live TV sat behind the floating bar, in the app and on the website on a phone, since 0.8.5. It now sits above the bar.
  • A phone on its side shows one top bar. Inside the app the website hid its own bars only on a phone held upright, so on its side the website’s top bar appeared under the app’s, with no room kept for the app’s floating bar. It now steps aside at any phone size.
  • Every button for the programme can be seen. On a phone they were a row that scrolled sideways, with half a button at the edge and Channel up, More about this programme and Day out of sight. They now wrap onto a second line.
  • 48 by 48 for a finger on Live TV’s buttons and the preview’s own. On a touch screen the preview’s bar keeps sound, full screen and close; the resize corner and its − and + are for a mouse.
  • The channel’s banner clears the player’s buttons on a phone on its side: one low band with the number, the programme and how far through it is, instead of a box drawn beneath −10 and play.
  • Live TV fills a phone’s screen. The page’s side margins and the space round Live TV’s panel were a frame of empty space round every part of it on a 412-wide phone. Upright, the preview is the screen’s width. A TV keeps its overscan margin.
  • The player’s −10 and play step aside while it shows a message (a channel that can’t be watched) or a break’s “We’ll be right back” card. They were drawn over the message and its buttons.
  • The layout check runs as the phone app shows a page (LAYOUT_APP=1, upright and on its side, Live TV’s full-screen player and a channel’s message included), in the smoke tests. It fails when Live TV doesn’t reach a phone’s sides.
  • Music visualizer: an effect hears the music after Bars. In Chrome, Edge and the desktop app, choosing a WittyVisualizer effect while Bars was showing gave an effect that moved but heard only silence, and it stayed silent for the next songs: Bars, handing over, switched off the copy of the sound the effect was listening to as well.
  • Music visualizer on a server without WittyVisualizer. An effect chosen earlier (or on another server) drew nothing at all there; the player’s own Bars now stand in. npm run test:visualizer checks every way into the full player (mini player first, straight away, Next, pause and play, Bars then an effect, no module).
  • Music visualizer: no empty screen while an effect loads the first time. Opened with an effect chosen, the full player showed nothing for the seconds the effect took to arrive from a new server; Bars now swell gently under it until it fades in.

Alpha software: it changes quickly and can fail or lose data. Keep backups, including your data folder, before you upgrade. No media comes with WittyMediaServer. Copy-protected discs, encrypted broadcasts and encrypted game files are refused with a plain message. Nothing here unlocks them.

This site has no cookies, trackers or analytics. Your choice of colours is kept in your own browser. How this site handles privacy. Change the colours.
