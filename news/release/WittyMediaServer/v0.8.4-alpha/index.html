WittyMediaServer 0.8.4-alpha

What the server says follows your language. Backup parts, Remote Access status, the peer-to-peer list, one-click

24 November 2026LatestOn GitHub

Release notes

What’s new in 0.8.4-alpha

Released 2026-11-24.

Languages (#497, #498)

  • What the server says follows your language. Backup parts, Remote Access status, the peer-to-peer list, one-click channels and every other sentence the server sends are now marked for translation and shown translated where a translation exists. German has the ones that were reported; the others follow in the translation pass. A check in CI stops new English slipping past.
  • Arabic and Hebrew keep Latin titles in order. A file name such as “00-aurora-…-2019” or a title’s “2 hr 10 min” no longer has its digits and dashes moved, and a long title is cut at its end. A screenshot test walks the pages in both languages.

Lighter on a small machine (#643)

  • An idle server on a small machine is about six times quieter. Go’s execution trace, kept for developers’ crash reports, woke it about 3,500 times a minute; it is now off by default on tiny and small machines (turn it on in Logs). On two processors: 0.04 % of one while idle, down from 0.25 %.
  • A server in a container held to two processors now counts two, not every processor of the machine it’s on, and holds itself back to match.
  • First scans of films are about a third quicker: the next films’ details are read while one is added.
  • The web app fetches its font once, not on every visit (a second visit to Home: 424 KB down to 24 KB), its first download is smaller, and scrolling a big library no longer draws every card again (2,219 renders for 1,500 films, down from 298,565).
  • New page: Minimum hardware (docs/MINIMUM-HARDWARE.md): what a Raspberry Pi, an old laptop and a NAS can do, and what needs a graphics card, from measurements on a two-processor container. Budgets for the web app’s download and the server’s memory, scans and pages are now checked on every change (docs/PERFORMANCE.md, “Budgets”).

Sign-in and other standalone pages (#624)

  • Sign in, Who’s watching, Sign in with your phone, can’t reach the server and the error page are one centred card with your server’s logo and name at the top. They fit a 1280×720 screen without scrolling, on a TV too.
  • Sign in with your phone has one heading and one sentence. With a QR code, the code and PIN sit beside it. On an address only this computer can use (127.0.0.1), there’s no QR code, which a phone couldn’t open, and the code leads.
  • The app’s “this address is for the app” page is no longer bare black text. It’s the same card, with a line on what to open instead.

First-time setup (#610, #550, #494)

  • Six plain steps instead of twelve: Welcome, Your account, Modules, Your media, Look, Finish. Each page has a short title, one sentence and one main button, with the usual answer already chosen. The language is on Welcome, the server’s name is on the account page, and Live TV and Games follow Your media when they’re installed.
  • Customize: every other choice (time zone, two-step sign-in, module updates, details beside your files, colours, the TV guide, the home network, posters and keys, logos, lyrics, karaoke voices, streaming lists) waits behind the step’s Customize button, already answered.
  • Advanced setup, on Welcome, goes through every choice a page at a time, next to the step it belongs with.
  • Finish offers Connect a device (the same code and QR code as Settings → Remote Access), adding a profile, and this screen as a TV, with the server’s address and Copy.
  • Fixed: picking a kind of library brings its form into view; the Games page counts a games library made anywhere; closing the modules dialog no longer stops installs; the browser tab follows the page; no module requests or warnings before the account exists.
  • Sign in: the server is named once, the page is one centred column, and a wrong password on a chosen profile says so (“That password isn’t right”).

Parental controls (#649)

  • Live TV is checked programme by programme. Antenna, cable and IPTV channels now go by the guide’s rating: a programme above a profile’s TV limit isn’t shown when tuning in, stops a stream when it comes on, and can’t be reached through catch-up. Library channels are held to the same. A programme the guide doesn’t rate counts as unrated.
  • Ratings country (Settings → Profiles): titles keep that country’s age rating, with the US one when it has none, and ratings are read as that country’s, since letters mean different ages in different places. Settings offers the boards of 21 more countries.
  • Explicit songs are known. A song’s explicit or clean label is read from its file when it is scanned, so an age limit keeps explicit songs from kids.
  • The parents’ PIN keeps a kids’ profile on its device. With Ask for the parents’ PIN to switch away or sign out, switching to another profile, signing out, or picking a profile with no password on that device’s sign-in screen asks for the PIN. A parent’s own password works too.
  • Limits, for a parent: a parent changes a kids’ profile’s screen time, bedtime and age on the child’s own device with the PIN.
  • New kids’ profiles start kid-safe: no explicit songs, no antenna or IPTV channels, at home only, no recording, downloads, sharing, comments, watch parties or playing on other devices, two hours of screen time on school days and three at weekends with a bedtime, and the PIN to leave. Profiles made before keep their limits.
  • A friendly “time’s up” page for kids’ profiles: one card with the server’s name, saying why and when they can watch again, with Ask a parent and Switch profile, on a computer, a phone and in TV mode.

Clone a station (#622)

  • A channel can follow a real TV station’s schedule, played from your own files. On Channels, press Clone a station, pick where the guide comes from (TVmaze’s free public schedule, an XMLTV link or file, or the guide your tuners and IPTV playlists already have), the days and the station. Every programme is matched to your library by series, season and episode, episode title, air date and metadata ids, with how sure each match is.
  • What you don’t have is filled in, and each slot says how: the closest episode of the same series, another episode of it, a similar show (genre, network, era, rating), or whatever fits the time. Green, amber and red show exact matches, stand-ins and gaps, and Swap chooses another in one press.
  • It keeps to the station’s clock, with commercials on by default: spread through each programme at its natural breaks where your files mark them, from the programme’s own era first. Advanced options has the matching rules, how many breaks, repeating, the time zone, a shift, and Follow the station, which reads the guide again each week. Only the guide’s words are used: nothing is recorded or decrypted. See docs/CHANNELS.md, “Clone a station”.

Connecting another device

  • A link or QR code works from away through your own WittyRelay (#614). A device that had never been at home couldn’t join when the server’s only way in was its own relay, which lets through only devices the server has listed. While an invite is open, the server now also lists a single-use key made from it, which the app uses only to show the invite; the relay still lets through no key the server hasn’t listed.

Players (#470)

  • A TV remote’s Up and Down no longer change the volume of a film. A TV has a volume of its own: on the picture, Up brings the controls back and Down goes into them, at the timeline. On a computer Up and Down still set the volume.
  • The quality menu never offers more than the original has. A 1080p film of 6.4 Mbps no longer lists “1080p, about 8 Mbps”; Original covers it, and the smaller sizes are held to the original’s rate.
  • Live TV on a phone gets the simple bar, as films do: channel up and down, the last channel, now and next and the guide are in its Options sheet. Live TV no longer has a speed setting.
  • Picture in picture is in the player’s More menu (and a phone’s Options sheet) where the browser has it. Not on a TV; the apps use their system’s own.
  • On a computer, the music bar no longer shows the page’s words in the gap under it.

Pages (#438)

  • WittyTV’s channel pages start where every other page starts, at the top and on the page’s left edge, not lower down in a narrower column of their own.
  • The chosen game on WittyPlay’s home rows stays inside its row. It grew twice, so its top ran under the tabs and the first one started off the left edge of the screen.

Libraries

  • An episode whose title starts with a number keeps it. “S01E05 - 80’s Cats” is episode 5, “80’s Cats”, not episodes 5 to 80 called “’s Cats”. Run Scan for changes to correct ones already scanned.

Settings, the same everywhere (#612)

  • One settings menu on every device. The web (computer and phone), its TV mode, the desktop app, and the Android and Apple apps show the same groups and rows, in the same order and words: the Android phone app’s You page. On a computer and a TV the person is on the left and the groups on the right, and each settings page has the same rows beside it. The list comes from the server (settingsMenu in its catalogue, docs/SETTINGS-MENU.md).
  • A TV shows every row, and says where to change the ones it can’t. Such a row stays in its place, says what is set (Remote access: Off, Live transcoding: Graphics chip…), and opens a panel with that setting’s own address and QR code to open on a computer or phone.
  • Module settings and Module store sit side by side under Modules (both were called Modules before, in two places), and each page links to the other. Look is now Layout, beside Edit layout and Theme.
  • Choose colours has no preview, only the theme cards, like Choose a layout.

Player, detail page and live TV (#634)

  • A click on the faded control bar brings it back. A click where the seek bar sits, a moment after the controls have faded, used to pause the film. Now it shows the controls instead.
  • A title’s languages are a short list. A film with 35 subtitle languages shows six, then “+25 more”, which shows the rest. The facts under a title sit in a grid with room between each label and its value.
  • A break between programmes is named as one. The guide and Home’s On TV now said “We’ll be right back” as if it were a programme. A gap is now “Short break” (or “Off air” for an hour or more), and “Next” always names the next programme.

Notifications (#654)

  • A bell beside Activity, for each profile: new episodes of shows the profile can watch, finished recordings, and for administrators failed scans, module updates and server news. Mark one or all read; in TV mode it’s the last pill in the bar. Settings → Notifications chooses which kinds each profile gets.
  • Email and webhooks for administrators, off until turned on, through your own mail server or a web address you enter. A backup made without a password leaves their addresses and secrets out.

Live TV: start over and time-shift (#655)

  • Start over, pause, rewind and skip breaks on library and custom channels (cloned stations too), for one viewer while the channel keeps running live for everyone else. Back to live is one button, and Skip break jumps a whole run of bumpers and commercials on a started-over programme. Parental limits apply to every one of them.
  • Start over on a phone is in the player’s Options sheet (#667).

Search (#645)

  • One search across every library: grouped by kind, typo-tolerant, recent searches per profile, voice where the device offers it.

Library health and backups (#646, #647)

  • Settings → Library health lists what needs a look (files that have gone, titles with no match, possible duplicates, odd names, NFO files and artwork) with the fix beside each.
  • Scheduled backups can go to a folder of your own, such as a second drive. A folder that isn’t there (a drive not plugged in) is never written to: the backup says why instead.
  • Pause for scenes: opening the scenes panel pauses the film on phones and TVs, or on every screen if you choose (#632).

Modules

  • A module that is only slow to start no longer looks like a crash in the log. When a module’s helper doesn’t say hello in time, the log now says the server stopped it, and says so when it wrote nothing, rather than “exit code 1” with nothing to go on.
  • A module that is too slow to start says so. The log reads “stopped by the server: no hello within 45s” with what the module wrote, instead of a bare “exit code 1” (#671).

Modules in this release: WittyPlay 0.7.18 (console photo switches a remote can reach; N64 homebrew and the graphics card check on Linux; games on phones).

WittyMediaClient 0.6.2 goes with this release: the same settings menu as the web, the notifications bell, the parents’ PIN to leave a kids’ profile, start over and time-shift, voice search, and new native player menus.
