New to media servers?

A plain guide for anyone who hasn’t run one before: what a media server is, what you need, and your first hour with WittyMediaServer.

DownloadQuestions and answers

The idea

Three parts, all in your home

If you’ve used a streaming service, this will feel familiar. The difference is that the films and music are your own, and so is the computer they’re kept on.

1. Your files

Films, shows, music, books and photos you already have, in folders on one computer or a NAS.

2. The server

WittyMediaServer runs on that computer. It sorts your files into libraries, finds posters and details, and remembers where everyone got to.

3. Your screens

Phones, tablets, laptops and TVs open the server in a web browser over your home network. The apps are optional.

No media comes with WittyMediaServer. It doesn’t sell, rent or stream films from anywhere else: it plays what you put in its folders.

Before you start

What you need

A computer that stays on

Windows 10 or 11, macOS 11 or newer, Linux, FreeBSD 14 or newer, or a NAS that runs Docker. It has to be on while anyone watches.

Your own media

Files you have the right to use. DVDs and Blu-rays play as disc images or folders. Encrypted discs are refused, and nothing here removes copy protection.

A browser on each screen

Nothing else to install there. The download for the server already includes FFmpeg, the program that converts video, and its database.

Internet, now and then

For posters and details, for adding modules and for updates. Watching at home doesn’t depend on it, and nothing needs an account with anyone.

How powerful a computer?

When a device can play a file as it is, the server only has to send it, which takes almost no work. When it can’t, the server converts the video as it plays, and that’s what takes real processor time. A graphics chip can do most of that work, which lets a small machine serve several people. Live transcoding explains the choices.

Step by step

Your first hour

  1. Download the server for the computer that holds your media, from the download page.
  2. Open it. On Windows and a Mac your browser opens the setup page. Elsewhere, go to http://<this computer>:9797.
  3. Make your account. It looks after libraries, people and settings. Keep the password safe: forgetting it is hard to undo.
  4. Choose what you want. Films, TV and music work straight away. Tick discs, games, photos or books to add their modules.
  5. Add a library. Pick a kind of media, such as Movies, and the folder it’s in. Titles appear as they’re found, and posters fill in after them.
  6. Press Play. Open a poster and press Play. Next time you’ll see Resume from.
  7. Try another screen. Type the server’s address, such as http://192.168.1.20:9797, into the browser on your TV or phone. Setup’s last page shows it.
  8. Add the family. Under Settings → Profiles, give each person a profile of their own.
Getting started guide

Worth doing first

Tidy folders get the right posters

The server finds videos in any layout, but a tidy one gets you the right titles. Keep films and shows in separate folders, put the year after a film’s title, and number episodes like S02E01:

Movies/
  The Long Harbour (2001)/The Long Harbour (2001).mkv
  Paper Lanterns (2014).mp4
TV Shows/
  Night Ferry/
    Season 02/
      Night Ferry - S02E01 - The Crossing.mkv

Libraries covers music, books, photos and every other kind.

Good habits

Start safely

Keep backups

A backup holds the server’s accounts, libraries and settings, not your media, so keep your own copy of your files too. See Backup and restore.

Stay at home first

The server only answers on your home network until you turn on Settings → Remote Access. The safest remote access is none, so read Remote access before you change that.

Expect an alpha

Alpha software: it changes quickly and can fail or lose data. Keep backups, including your data folder, before you upgrade.

Read before you add

Modules from this project are signed and checked. A store or fork from someone else is their code on your server, so only add one you trust.

Only your own media

Add files you have the right to use, and keep the server to people you trust. The legal notes say who’s responsible for what.

Ask when stuck

Troubleshooting goes by what you’re seeing, and Discussions on GitHub is the place for questions.

Words you’ll see

A short glossary

Every guide uses these the same way.

WordWhat it means
Media serverA program that keeps your films, shows, music and more in one place, and plays them on any screen in your home.
Self-hostedIt runs on a computer you own, not on someone else’s. Nobody else holds your library.
LibraryOne or more folders holding one kind of media, such as Movies or Music.
ScanThe server looking through a library’s folders for new or changed files.
MetadataThe details around a file: its poster, plot, cast, year and rating. The server looks them up for you.
ProfileEach person’s own place on the server, with their own progress and favourites. Kids profiles show only titles up to the age you choose.
ModuleA part of the server you can add or remove, such as disc menus or games.
Direct playThe file is sent as it is, with nothing converted. It’s the full quality, and it takes almost no work from the server.
TranscodingConverting a video while it plays, so a device that can’t play the original can still show it. It uses the server’s processor or graphics chip.
Disc imageOne file holding a whole disc, usually an .iso. The server plays it like a disc player, menus and all.
NFOA small text file beside a video that holds its title, plot and year.
Remote accessReaching your server from outside your home. It’s off until you turn it on.
AlphaAn early version. It works, but it changes quickly and can fail or lose data.

Ready when you are

Install the server, open its page in a browser, and point it at a folder of films. Setup takes a few minutes.
