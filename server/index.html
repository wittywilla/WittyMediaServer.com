The server

WittyMediaServer

Self-hosted software that keeps your films, shows, discs, music, books and games on a computer you own, and streams them to every screen in your home. It plays DVDs and Blu-rays with their real menus, in any browser.

0.8.3-alphaReleased 24 November 2026AGPL-3.0-or-later
DownloadGetting started guideGitHub

Install it on the computer that holds your media. Every other screen reaches it through a web browser, with no app needed. Discs, games, photos, books and TV tuners are modules you add from the server’s store. The server works without any of them.

Films and TV

Your films and shows, looked after

Posters, plots, cast and ratings arrive with no API key, and you can add free keys or your own sources for more. Everything you watch picks up where you left off, on any screen.

  • Continue watching and instant seeking
  • Skip intros, recaps, credits and adverts, found from chapter names or a file beside the video
  • Collections, including smart ones that fill themselves
  • Converts video only when a device needs it

Discs

DVDs and Blu-rays, with their real menus

With the WittyDVD and WittyBluray modules, disc images and folders play like a disc player: the intro, the animated menu, scene selection, extras, and the disc’s own audio and subtitles. No mounting, no ripping to files first. Blu-rays have been tested on custom-made discs, so retail discs may show problems not seen yet.

  • DVD images and VIDEO_TS folders, with menus, chapters and angles
  • Blu-ray images and BDMV folders, including 4K and HDR
  • Audio CD images, played gapless in an on-screen CD player
  • Play movie plays the film the disc modules rank first, with Why this title and how sure they are
  • Copy-protected discs are refused with a plain message. Nothing unlocks them

Live TV and channels

Channels made from your own library

WittyTV turns your libraries into channels in one press, with a guide that looks like real TV, and needs no extra hardware. Add the WittyTuner module for live TV and recording from network tuners, IPTV playlists and Xtream Codes-style providers. The guide pages through channels on the server, 25 to 100 at a time or all of them scrolling, finds a channel by name or number, and stays quick with ten thousand. IPTV has been tested with local streams and Xtream with a stand-in panel; tuners and capture cards haven’t been tried on real hardware yet.

  • Sets: all channels, favourites, one source or group, or your own, renamed, reordered and shared
  • Watch from the start, on channels whose source keeps an archive
  • A health check of every channel, and backups tried by themselves when a channel won’t open
  • Series recordings by title on any channel: new episodes only, keep the newest few, start early or end late
  • Encrypted channels are refused, never decrypted

Music and audiobooks

Everything you listen to

Albums and artists, internet radio, podcasts you follow, and audiobooks that remember your place. Sing along with lyrics and karaoke, or watch WittyVisualizer’s light shows. Opening karaoke on a song makes a version with the singer turned down by itself, and a properly separated one when Demucs, Spleeter or audio-separator is installed on the server.

  • Albums, artists and playlists
  • Lyrics, and karaoke with a Vocals slider
  • Podcasts, downloaded for you
  • Audiobooks that remember your place

Books and comics

A reader for every kind of book

Read EPUB books, PDFs, comics and manga in the browser, with highlights, notes, definitions and a pen. Your place follows you from screen to screen.

  • EPUB with book view and typography
  • PDFs with selectable text, outline and search
  • Comics and manga, in either reading direction
  • Highlights, notes and handwriting

Photos and videos

Photos, and the videos people make

A timeline of your photos with albums and an offline map, a feed of vertical videos you swipe through, and creators’ videos grouped by who made them.

  • A timeline, albums and an offline map
  • RAW previews and camera details
  • Shorts in a feed you swipe through
  • Creator videos, music videos and concerts by channel

Games

Your own games, played in a browser

WittyPlay runs your game files on the server and streams them to the page, so a phone or a TV can play them with a gamepad, keyboard or touch. GameCube, Wii, Wii U, PlayStation 3 and Xbox games are recognised from their own files, and play in standalone emulators in Docker, in Linux’s sandbox, or on a Mac’s or Windows PC’s own desktop, with every emulator’s settings in the web interface. The Switch and Switch 2 play only in Ryujinx or Lichii, which you install and add yourself. WittyCouch plays games on a PC of your own that runs Sunshine. None of this has been tried with a real controller or a real gaming PC yet, and the standalone emulators haven’t played a game on a desktop or a real Docker host yet.

  • Games run on the server and stream to any screen
  • Standalone emulators with their settings in Settings, controllers set up for up to four players
  • Settings → What this server plays says which consoles are ready
  • You bring the games, and any keys or firmware. None come with it, and nothing is decrypted

Everyone at home

One server, everyone’s own place

Each person gets their own progress, lists and settings, and the screens at home work together.

Who’s watching?

Profiles with their own progress, favourites, game saves and picture. Switch profile on a shared screen without signing out, with that profile’s password or PIN.

Parental controls

An age for films, TV, games, music and books in your country’s ratings, collections and tags to allow or hide, and switches for games, recording, downloads and more. Ratings can be missing or wrong, so it’s a help, not a guarantee.

Screen time

A daily allowance for school days and weekends, bedtimes, and “ask a parent” for more with a parents’ PIN. The server counts the time and stops what’s playing.

Watch parties

Start one from a title’s page and share its six-letter code. Everyone’s player stays in step, wherever they are, and nobody sees what their limits don’t allow.

Play on another screen

Send what’s playing to another signed-in device and carry on at the same place, or steer it from your phone: pause, seek, volume, tracks and a TV’s arrow keys.

Sign in with your phone

A TV shows a QR code and a PIN that changes every 30 seconds. Approve it from a phone that’s signed in, with no password typed on the TV.

Also new in 0.8.0-alpha

From anywhere, on any machine

From anywhere, peer to peer

Turn on peer-to-peer connections and the apps reach your server from anywhere, with no port forwarding, no rented server and no account. Encrypted to your server’s key, and only for devices you approved.

How it works

Small machines

On a Raspberry Pi 3, an old Celeron or a container with little memory, the server keeps to about a quarter of the memory, reads and makes fewer pictures at once, and lets scans give way to what’s playing.

Karaoke that sets itself up

Open karaoke on a song and a version with the singer turned down is ready in seconds, with a properly separated one behind it when Demucs, Spleeter or audio-separator is installed. Nothing to configure.

Make it yours

Pages arranged on the server, drawn by every app

Home, your libraries, a title’s page, Search and Live TV are described by the server and drawn by each app, so a page arranged on the server looks the same in a browser, the desktop app and the Android and Apple apps.

Customise this page

At the foot of a page: reorder, hide and pin its rows, and choose spacing, card sizes and the tab it opens on. It follows your profile to every device, with Undo and Reset.

Pages and the menu

Settings → Pages & menu lets an administrator build pages and the menu for everyone from ready-made pieces, try a draft in a phone, tablet, desktop or TV preview, then publish, with history to go back to.

Layouts and colours

Five layouts (Sidebar, Top tabs, TV rows, Compact list and Streaming style), set for each kind of device and chosen per device by each profile. The server’s themes reach every page, with a module’s own accent and a profile’s own colours, refused when text would be hard to read.

Settings stays each app’s own, in the same place and style as before. A profile can be kept from rearranging pages.

Modules

Start small, add what you want

Modules add what your server can do, such as DVD menus, streamed games or live TV. Most are projects of their own, signed, and added from Settings → Modules while the server keeps running.

A module built for module API 4 carries its own pages, rows for Home, menu entries, settings and colours, as data the server checks before it uses them. Every app that draws the server’s pages draws the module’s too, with nothing of the module built into the app.

  • Signed, and checked before they run
  • Added and updated without a restart, from a store you can search and filter by kind
  • Installing past a rule (an unfinished version, one from a file, one unsigned) is your choice to make, with the warning, your password again, and an Overridden label you can take back
  • Removing one leaves your media and history as they are

Where it runs

Platforms and architectures

These are the files in 0.8.3-alpha. amd64 is most PCs; arm64 is Apple silicon and many small boards. Linux gets the most testing. The Windows installer has been tested under Wine, not on real Windows. The FreeBSD package has run in virtual machines, not on real hardware. The Mac installer has never been run, and neither have the AppImage, Flatpak and snap.

Any architecture your host supports

FFmpeg comes with every download except FreeBSD on arm64, which needs pkg install ffmpeg. Phones, tablets and TVs need none of these: open the server’s address in a browser, or use WittyMediaClient.

Alpha software: it changes quickly and can fail or lose data. Keep backups, including your data folder, before you upgrade. No media comes with WittyMediaServer. Copy-protected discs, encrypted broadcasts and encrypted game files are refused with a plain message. Nothing here unlocks them. Legal notes.

Try it on a computer you already have

Install the server, open its page in a browser, and point it at a folder of films. Setup takes a few minutes.
